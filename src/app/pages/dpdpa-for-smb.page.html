FAQ

Frequently asked questions

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 governs how any organisation processing digital personal data of individuals in India must collect, use, store and delete it. It applies regardless of company size — there is no small-business exemption. If you hold customer names, emails, phone numbers or payment details, you are a Data Fiduciary under the Act.

The Act received assent in August 2023 and is being brought into force in phases through subordinate rules. Organisations are being given a transition window before penalty provisions bite. Because remediation typically takes 4–6 months, most boards are treating readiness as a now problem rather than a later one. We will confirm the current enforcement position for your sector during the assessment.

The DPDP Act provides for financial penalties of up to ₹250 crore per contravention, with the Data Protection Board of India determining the amount based on the nature, gravity and duration of the breach. Failure to implement reasonable security safeguards and failure to notify a breach are both separately penalisable.

The readiness assessment itself takes about 30 minutes and gives you a score plus a gap list the same day. A typical SMB implementation — consent, RoPA, policies, DPR handling, breach playbook and vendor governance — runs 4–6 months depending on how many systems hold personal data.

A DPO is mandatory for Significant Data Fiduciaries as notified by the government. Other organisations still need a contactable person responsible for grievance redressal under Section 13. XcellHost offers a virtual DPO advisory retainer that covers the role, quarterly reviews and board reporting without a full-time hire.
