info

The text behind a DNS name

Policies and verification data live alongside address records.

TXT records let a domain publish text for other systems to read. Several records can coexist at the same name, and each record can contain one or more strings. Their meaning depends on the service or protocol using them.

This tool asks the public resolver you select for TXT records at one hostname. It does not enumerate the domain’s other names, verify ownership with a provider or evaluate an email message’s authentication.

SPF

A sending policy commonly published at the email domain.

DKIM · DMARC

Mail-signing keys and domain policies, normally at dedicated hostnames.

VERIFICATION

Tokens that services can use to check control of a DNS name.

checklist

Read a TXT snapshot in four steps

Start with the exact name your service expects.

  1. Enter a domain or subdomain

    Use the actual TXT hostname. DMARC and DKIM records are usually not at the root domain.

  2. Select a resolver and check

    Choose Google Public DNS or Cloudflare in Advanced settings, then run the TXT query.

  3. Inspect the complete values

    Use the purpose filters and compare joined text with the original DNS response.

  4. Keep a copy of your findings

    Copy individual values or export PDF, CSV and JSON with the lookup context.
