How signed DNS answers earn trust

Public keys, delegation records and signatures work together.

DNSSEC lets a validating resolver check the authenticity of DNS data using signatures and a configured trust anchor. A signed zone publishes DNSKEY records, while DS records in its parent connect the delegation to a zone key. RRSIG records carry signatures over individual record sets.

This check queries an A answer and discovers the relevant zone, then inspects its DNSKEY and DS records with DNSSEC data requested. The resolver's AD flag supplies the validation result. Local record parsing, digest comparison and time-window checks help explain the evidence without claiming an independent audit of the complete chain.

DNSKEY

A zone's public key and signing-related flags.

DS

A parent-zone digest identifying a child key.

RRSIG

A signature covering a particular record set.

Technical references: DNSSEC record formats · Google DNS-over-HTTPS flags.
