What is a WHOIS lookup?
A WHOIS lookup shows the public registration record of a domain name: the registrar it was bought through, when it was registered and when it expires, its status codes (such as clientTransferProhibited), its nameservers and, where not privacy-protected, the registrant's organisation and country. Today most registries publish this through RDAP, the structured successor to WHOIS. XcellHost's free tool queries RDAP and live DNS so you get registration details and current nameservers in one search.
What is the difference between WHOIS and RDAP?
WHOIS is the original plain-text protocol for domain registration data. RDAP (Registration Data Access Protocol) is its modern replacement: structured JSON, secure HTTPS, standardised fields and support for privacy redaction. ICANN now requires gTLD registries and registrars to support RDAP, and this tool uses it.
Why are the owner's name and email hidden?
Many registrars redact personal contact data by default to comply with privacy laws such as GDPR and India's DPDP Act, or because the owner bought privacy protection. You'll usually still see the registrar, dates, status and nameservers, and the registrar's abuse contact.
Can I look up .in domains?
Yes. .in, .co.in and other Indian domains are served by the .IN registry's RDAP service, alongside .com, .net, .org and most other extensions.
How do I find out when a domain expires?
Search the domain above — the expiry date is shown in the result. If it's your domain, set auto-renew at your registrar or ask XcellHost to manage renewals for you.
The domain I want is taken. What can I do?
Check its expiry date and status: if it's in redemptionPeriod or pendingDelete it may become available. Otherwise contact the owner through the registrar, or choose another extension such as .in, .co.in or .com.
What does it mean if no record is found?
Usually that the domain isn't registered — so it may be available. Some country-code registries don't publish RDAP, in which case we show live DNS only. Try registering it or ask us to check.
