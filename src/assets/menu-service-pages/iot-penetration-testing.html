The problem

Devices shipped with default or hardcoded credentials
Firmware updates that are neither signed nor encrypted
Sensor and user data sent in clear text over the air
One weak device becoming a doorway into the whole network

The solution

An end-to-end penetration test of the device and everything around it. Our testers simulate real attacks on the hardware, firmware, communication and cloud layers, then report each weakness with its risk, its impact and a fix.

Definition

What is IoT device penetration testing?

It means simulating real-world cyberattacks to find vulnerabilities in your IoT devices and the ecosystem around them. The assessment examines device firmware, communication protocols and server-side components, with one goal: preventing data breaches, unauthorised access and service disruption.

The device itself

Debug ports, storage, boot process and firmware are examined for ways in.

How it communicates

Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, cellular and MQTT traffic is captured and tested.

What it reports to

Cloud APIs, dashboards and companion apps are tested alongside the device.

Five layers in scope

Whole-ecosystem coverage
  • Hardware and physical interfaces
  • Firmware and update mechanism
  • Wireless and network protocols
  • Cloud platform and APIs
  • Mobile and web companion apps
What we look for

Common IoT device vulnerabilities

Manual penetration testing identifies weaknesses across the device and its ecosystem.

01

Weak authentication mechanisms

Default passwords, hardcoded credentials and login flows that can be bypassed or brute-forced.

02

Insecure firmware updates

Updates delivered without signing, encryption or rollback protection.

03

Insufficient data encryption

Sensitive data stored on the device or sent across the network without adequate protection.

04

Poor access control policies

Users, devices or services able to reach functions and data beyond their role.

05

Unsecured communication protocols

Plain-text MQTT, weak Bluetooth pairing and replayable radio commands.

06

Weak cloud interface security

Cloud dashboards and APIs that expose other customers’ devices or data.

07

Lack of device hardening

Open debug ports, unnecessary services and unprotected boot loaders.

08

Privacy concerns

Personal data collected, retained or shared beyond what users agreed to.

09

Insecure third-party integrations

Vulnerable libraries, SDKs and partner services built into the product.

Testing modes

Types of IoT device penetration testing

The approach is chosen to fit your ecosystem.

01
Zero knowledge

Black box testing

We act as an outside attacker with no inside knowledge, testing the device’s real-world defences against unknown threats.

02
Full knowledge

White box testing

We work with full access to firmware, hardware schematics, APIs and source code to uncover hidden vulnerabilities and logic flaws.

03
Some knowledge

Grey box testing

A blend of both using limited internal information — broad insight while mimicking a partly informed attacker.
