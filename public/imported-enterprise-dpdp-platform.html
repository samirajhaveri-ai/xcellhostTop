Why now

The DPDP Act is law. The core duties start on 13 May 2027.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025 apply to any business that handles the digital personal data of people in India. You need a notice people can read, consent you can show, a way to answer rights requests, security safeguards, and a plan to report a breach within hours. XcellPrivacy keeps all of that in one place, with evidence attached.

  • Notice and consent in English or any Eighth Schedule language the person chooses (§5(3)).
  • Rights requests answered within the period the Rules set: at most 90 days for grievances.
  • Breach intimation to the Board and each affected person; detailed report to the Board within 72 hours.
  • Penalties up to ₹250 crore per instance for failing to take reasonable security safeguards.
Commencement timeline— days to 13 May 2027
  1. 11 Aug 2023DPDP Act, 2023 enacted.
  2. 13 Nov 2025DPDP Rules notified. Definitions and Data Protection Board provisions in force.
  3. 13 Nov 2026Consent Manager registration and duties apply (one year after notification).
  4. 13 May 2027Notice, consent, security, breach, retention, rights, children's data and SDF duties apply (18 months).

Dates from the Rules as notified and PIB releases of 14 Nov and 3 Dec 2025; checked November 2026.

            The DPDP Act, 2023 applies to anyone processing digital personal data in India, and to processing outside India linked to offering goods or services to people in India. From 13 May 2027 you must give clear notices, hold valid consent or another lawful ground, protect the data, answer rights requests and report breaches. Customers, investors and enterprise buyers are already asking for proof.
            
      
            A lawyer interprets the law and drafts your notices and contracts; you still need them. XcellPrivacy produces the evidence your counsel needs. It is not legal advice.
            
      
            It finds where personal data lives, shows customers a notice in their language and records what they agree to, lets them ask for their data or its deletion and tracks the deadline, and helps you report a breach on time. Each step leaves a sealed record you can show.

Discovery across many systems and a full set of records of processing take longer, depending on how quickly access is granted. We give you a dated plan after the scoping call.
            
      
            The Data Protection Board can impose penalties per instance under the Schedule to the Act: up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, up to ₹200 crore for not notifying a breach or for breaching duties on children's data, up to ₹150 crore for Significant Data Fiduciary duties, and up to ₹50 crore for other breaches. The Board weighs gravity, duration and mitigation when setting the amount.

A GDPR programme is a good start, but it needs re-mapping.
            
      
            Yes, there is no size threshold in the Act. Section 17(3) lets the Government notify certain Data Fiduciaries, including startups, as exempt from some duties (notice under §5, §8(3), §8(7), §10 and §11). Check whether any such notification covers you before relying on it; we assume no exemption by default. Starter is sized for small teams.
