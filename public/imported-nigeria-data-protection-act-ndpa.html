Scope & reach · NDPA s.2

Three ways in. The third one reaches India.

President Tinubu signed the Nigeria Data Protection Act on 12 June 2023. It covers automated and manual processing, and section 2(2) brings in three groups of controllers and processors.

s.2(2)(a)

Based in Nigeria

The controller or processor is domiciled, resident or operating in Nigeria — a subsidiary, branch or office.

s.2(2)(b)

Processing in Nigeria

The processing itself happens in Nigeria — a Lagos data centre, a local BPO, a field team.

s.2(2)(c)

Data subjects in Nigeria

A controller or processor outside Nigeria processes personal data of a data subject in Nigeria. An Indian SaaS, fintech, edtech or BPO with Nigerian users or clients is in.

What this means for Indian companies

  • Serving Nigerian customers from India brings the principles, lawful bases, rights, security, breach and transfer rules with it.
  • NDPC registration and audit returns attach to controllers of major importance, which s.65 defines as those domiciled, resident or operating in Nigeria — so a Nigerian entity or office usually triggers them. Confirm with Nigerian counsel.
  • Processing for a Nigerian bank, telco or insurer makes you their processor: expect NDPA clauses, audits and breach duties in the contract.
  • Data moved from Nigeria to India is a cross-border transfer that needs a recorded basis.

What the Act leaves out (s.3)

  • Purely personal or household processing.
  • Law enforcement, national security, public-health emergencies, journalism, research and legal claims are exempt from most of Part V — but ss.24, 25, 32 and 40 still apply.
  • The NDPC can add exemptions by regulation and issue guidance on safeguards.

Source: Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, ss.2, 3 and 65. Signing date: KPMG Nigeria and Mondaq, June–August 2023.

Regulator & tiers · checked 11 Nov 2026

The NDPC, the GAID and the three tiers.

The Act created the Nigeria Data Protection Commission. Its General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID), issued 20 March 2025 and effective 19 September 2025, sets out how the Act applies in practice — including who counts as a controller or processor "of major importance" (DCPMI).

What the NDPC can do

  • Register DCPMIs and set fees and levies (ss.5, 44, 45).
  • Issue regulations and directives — the GAID is one (s.6).
  • License Data Protection Compliance Organisations (DPCOs) to audit and report on compliance (s.33).
  • Investigate complaints and call for information (s.46).
  • Issue compliance and enforcement orders, including compensation and penalties (ss.47–48).

Not following a compliance order is an offence under s.49, with a fine and/or up to one year's imprisonment.

    Core duties · sections of the Act

    Twelve duties, each with its section number.

    Filter by theme. Each card cites the section of the NDPA 2023 and, where it adds detail, the GAID 2025 article.

    s.24

    Six principles

    Fair, lawful and transparent; purpose limitation; minimisation; storage limitation; accuracy; security — plus accountability you can show (s.24(3)).

    s.25

    Six lawful bases

    Consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public interest and legitimate interests. GAID Art. 26 expects a written legitimate-interest assessment first.

    ss.26, 35

    Consent you can prove

    Affirmative, specific and informed — no pre-ticked boxes, silence is not consent. Withdrawal must be as easy as giving it; the controller carries the burden of proof.

    s.27

    Privacy notice

    Identity, basis, purposes, recipients, retention, rights, automated decisions and the right to complain to the NDPC — before collection.

    s.31

    Children

    Parent or guardian consent, with age and consent verification; government-approved ID is acceptable. "Child" follows the Child's Rights Act.

    s.30

    Sensitive data

    Genetic and biometric, race or ethnic origin, religion, health, sex life, political opinions and trade-union membership — only on a s.30 condition such as explicit consent.

    s.34

    Access, correction, erasure

    Copies of data, correction, deletion and restriction on request, with the information the Act lists.

    ss.36–38

    Object, automated decisions, portability

    An absolute right to object to direct marketing; no solely automated decisions with significant effects without safeguards; data portability.

    s.32

    Data Protection Officer

    Required for DCPMIs; employee or contractor with expert knowledge. GAID Arts. 11–14: publish contact details, send them to the NDPC, report semi-annually, certify annually.

    s.28

    DPIA

    Before high-risk processing; consult the NDPC if high risk remains. GAID Art. 28 lists triggers, including fintech, health, e-commerce, education and cross-border transfers.

    ss.24(2), 39

    Security measures

    Appropriate technical and organisational measures — pseudonymisation, encryption, resilience and regular testing — scaled to risk, volume and harm.

    s.40

    Breach notification

    Processor tells the controller; controller tells the NDPC within 72 hours if there is risk, and data subjects immediately if risk is high. Record every breach.

        Banks, telcos and government answer to more than one rule.

        In force

        CBN Risk-Based Cybersecurity Framework

        Deposit money banks and payment service banks, from 1 July 2024: monitoring, outsourced or in-house response, CBN approval for emerging tech, and incidents reported to the CBN within 24 hours.

        In force

        Cybercrimes (Amendment) Act 2024
