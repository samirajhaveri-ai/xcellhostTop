    Overview

    A penetration test finds the holes. A red team finds out whether anyone is watching them.

    Most organisations have scanned and pen-tested their systems. Far fewer have checked what happens when someone patient, quiet and goal-driven tries to reach the one system that would really hurt — the payments platform, the customer database, the plant historian, the board's files. That is the question a red team answers.

    XcellHost Red Team Assessment is an authorised, objective-based exercise. Together with a small white team on your side, we agree the objectives, the rules of engagement and the testing windows. Our red team then emulates a realistic adversary across people, process and technology, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK tactics — while your SOC defends as it would on any normal day. At the end we put both timelines side by side and replay the key moments with your defenders.

    Objective firstSuccess is reaching an agreed flag, not counting vulnerabilities.
    Detection measuredEvery phase is timed against when your team saw it.
    Governed end to endWritten authorisation, RoE, NDA and a stop switch.
    Ends in learningA replay with your blue team turns findings into detections.
    Why it matters in India

    Attackers don't stop at the first vulnerability. Your testing shouldn't either.

    Indian organisations now have a six-hour window to report serious incidents to CERT-In, a duty under the DPDP Act 2023 to protect personal data with reasonable safeguards, and sector regulators asking for evidence that defences are tested, not just bought. A red team is the closest you can get to a real incident without having one.

    • Detection speed is the real control. The gap between an attacker's first step and your first alert decides whether you report a near-miss or a breach.
    • People and process get tested too. Escalation paths, on-call handovers and approval workflows are where real incidents stall.
    • Tools overlap and gaps hide between them. EDR, SIEM, email security and identity protection each see part of the story.

    Governance & safety

    Realistic for your defenders. Controlled for your business.

    A red team only works if leadership trusts it. Every exercise runs under a written framework that a small white team on your side controls from start to finish.

    White team2–4 people · your side · aware of the exercise

    Usually the CISO, an IT lead and a legal or risk owner. They approve each phase, take deconfliction calls if your SOC escalates, and can pause or stop the exercise at any moment.

    Written authorisationA signed letter naming the organisation, systems, dates and people — nothing starts without it.
    Rules of engagementIn-scope and out-of-scope systems, allowed and forbidden methods, escalation contacts and stop conditions.
    Safe testing windowsSensitive steps only in agreed windows; business-critical periods such as month-end are blocked out.
    No production impactNo destructive actions, no service disruption, no changes left behind. Test accounts and artefacts are removed at close.
    Data handlingFlags are synthetic. Evidence is encrypted, kept only for the agreed period and then destroyed with a certificate.
    NDA & named teamA mutual NDA, and every tester named to the white team before the exercise begins.

    India compliance mapping

    Evidence your auditors and regulators recognise — without overclaiming.

    A red team does not make you compliant on its own. It produces dated, independent evidence that helps you meet these expectations.

    CERT-In directions (April 2022)

    Tests whether you would spot and escalate an incident in time for the 6-hour reporting window, and whether the 180 days of logs you must keep actually reconstruct what the red team did.

    DPDP Act 2023

    Checks the reasonable security safeguards that protect personal data. Our flags are synthetic, so the exercise itself creates no personal-data exposure.

    RBI · SEBI CSCRF · IRDAI

    Sector frameworks expect periodic, independent testing of defences and response — for some regulated entities including red-team exercises. We map the evidence to your category.

    MeitY & ISO 27001

    Supports government-sector security expectations and the ISO 27001 controls for technical vulnerability management and incident response testing.

    Use cases by industry

    Different businesses, different crown jewels.

    Typical objectives we agree with clients. Yours are set in the scoping workshop.

    Banks, NBFCs and insurers

    Regulators expect tested defences, and attackers target payment and approval workflows. Exercises often run assumed-breach from a branch workstation.

    • Approve a payment in the test approval workflow
    • Reach the core-banking jump host
    • Read a synthetic file on the treasury share

    Fintech and payment companies

    Cloud-native estates where identity and CI/CD access are the real perimeter.

    • Obtain a privileged cloud role in a non-production account
    • Reach the build pipeline's deployment secrets store
    • Read a synthetic record from the merchant database

    Hospitals and diagnostics

    Shared workstations, many vendors and sensitive health data under the DPDP Act.

    • Reach the patient-records application with a test identity
    • Move from the guest network to the clinical VLAN
    • Prove access to imaging storage with a marker file

    Manufacturers and plants

    The IT/OT boundary is the objective — OT systems themselves are observed, never touched. Pair with an OT assessment.

    • Reach the IT/OT boundary jump server
    • Read the plant historian's read-only replica
    • Obtain a vendor remote-access account

    IT services and SaaS

    Customer trust depends on tenant isolation and source code protection.

    • Reach a synthetic customer tenant from a staff laptop
    • Gain write access to a source repository
    • Obtain a support-desk administrator session

    Retail and e-commerce

    Large store and warehouse footprints, seasonal peaks and payment data.

    • Reach the order-management back office
    • Move from a store network to head-office servers
    • Read a synthetic customer list from the CRM
