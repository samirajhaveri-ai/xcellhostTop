    Overview · what is a private cloud?

    The flexibility of cloud, on servers nobody else touches.

    A private cloud is a cloud platform built for one organisation. You get self-service virtual machines, snapshots, templates and private networks — the parts of cloud people like — but the physical servers, storage and network switches are dedicated to you. No other customer's workload shares your CPUs, memory or disks.

    XcellHost builds it as a cluster of three or more dedicated nodes in our Tier IV data centre in Mumbai, with the hypervisor you choose, storage replicated across nodes and high availability switched on. We patch, monitor and back it up; you run your applications. Prices are a fixed monthly figure in INR, with a GST invoice — no per-hour meter and no egress surprises.

    How it works · architecture

    From your office to your VMs, every hop is yours.

    Follow the packet. Users reach the cluster over the internet, a site-to-site VPN or a dedicated link; the firewall passes them to your private network; the hypervisor runs the VM; storage keeps three copies; backup copies leave the building.

    01 · Connect

    Internet with DDoS filtering, IPsec site-to-site VPN, or a dedicated link from your office.

    02 · Filter

    Perimeter firewall and per-network rules decide what may talk to what.

    03 · Run

    Your VMs on dedicated nodes; if one fails, HA restarts its VMs on the others.

    04 · Store & protect

    Each write kept as three copies across nodes; backups copied out of the cluster.

    CERT-In log retention

    CERT-In's April 2022 directions ask organisations to keep ICT system logs for 180 days within India and to report notified cyber incidents within 6 hours. We keep cluster, firewall and access logs in India and our SOC helps you prepare the report.

    AES-256 at restTLS 1.3 in transitCertified erasure on exit

    Asks data fiduciaries for reasonable security safeguards and prompt breach intimation. Dedicated hardware, encryption and access logging are safeguards you can document.

    The physical servers (nodes), their disks and the storage pool are reserved for you alone. Your VMs never share CPU, memory or disks with another XcellHost customer. Shared facility items — power, cooling, the building and upstream internet — are common, as in any data centre.

    Three nodes let the cluster keep a majority vote (quorum) when one node fails, keep three copies of storage on separate servers and restart a failed node's VMs on the other two. Fewer nodes can't do all three safely.
