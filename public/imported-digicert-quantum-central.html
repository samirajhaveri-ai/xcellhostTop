    Protocols & algorithms

    TLS 1.3 and hybrid ML-KEM first, signatures and PKI next.

    The practical order DigiCert recommends — and the one XcellHost's migration plans follow.

    Hybrid key exchangeillustrative

    Hybrid pairs a classical algorithm (RSA or ECC) with a post-quantum one, so protection holds if either is broken. Per DigiCert, deploy hybrid key exchange on external TLS now, with a rollback path.

    FIPS 203

    ML-KEM

    NIST's key-encapsulation standard for key exchange. DigiCert notes browsers, CDNs, IIS and Apache support it and major browsers and clouds already use it in hybrid mode — the first algorithm to deploy.

    FIPS 204

    ML-DSA

    NIST's lattice-based signature standard. Signatures are larger than RSA or ECC, which can affect chain size, IoT devices and high-volume systems — pilot first.

    FIPS 205

    SLH-DSA

    A hash-based signature standard published alongside the other two in August 2024, giving a second, differently-built signature option.

    TLS 1.3

    The route to hybrid

    The IETF is not adding post-quantum key exchange to TLS 1.2, so TLS 1.2-only systems need a protocol upgrade. Checking TLS versions is an early, cheap win.

    Why it matters in India

    Long-lived Indian data, existing rules that already ask for evidence.

    No Indian regulator has been cited by DigiCert with a PQC deadline. But the inventory, ownership and logging that Quantum Central produces helps you meet obligations you already have.

    DPDP Act, 2023

    Personal data must be protected with reasonable security safeguards. Knowing which systems still use quantum-vulnerable encryption for long-retained personal data is part of showing those safeguards are reasonable.

    CERT-In directions (April 2022)

    Report cyber incidents within 6 hours and keep ICT logs for a rolling 180 days in India. XcellHost runs the managed service from India and keeps programme logs and change records accordingly.

    RBI & SEBI cyber frameworks

    Banks, NBFCs and market intermediaries face IT and cyber-security reviews that ask for asset inventories and cryptographic controls — a CBOM and a dated roadmap are strong evidence.

    IT Act, 2000

    Electronic signatures and records rest on public-key cryptography; planning for ML-DSA-era signatures keeps signed records verifiable over their legal life.

    Products customers will ask about

    SBOMs and CBOMs show which libraries and ciphers ship in your product. Plan crypto-agility in code, test ML-KEM in your TLS stack and track signing keys for releases.

    • SBOM / CBOM ingestion
    • Library-level findings
    • Code-signing keys tracked

    Health and policy records that last decades

    Patient histories and insurance claims are prime harvest-now targets. Prioritise portals, HL7/FHIR interfaces and backups that carry long-retained personal data under the DPDP Act.

    • Long-lived data first
    • Backup encryption reviewed
    • DPDP safeguards evidence

    Devices that outlive the algorithms

    Meters, controllers and connected products ship for 10–15 years. Inventory device identities and firmware-signing keys now, and test ML-DSA on constrained hardware before the next design cycle.

    • Device PKI inventory
    • Firmware signing plan
    • Performance pilots

    Larger post-quantum keys and signatures matter at scale. Pilot hybrid TLS on a representative service, measure handshake cost, and ask network vendors for their PQC roadmaps.
