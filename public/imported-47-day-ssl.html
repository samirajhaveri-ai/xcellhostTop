Overview

Shorter certificates are already here — the renewal work is about to multiply.

The industry vote is done and the dates are fixed. What changes is how often your team has to get every renewal right.

Every website, API, mail server and load balancer you run behind HTTPS uses a public SSL/TLS certificate with an expiry date. In April 2025 the browsers and certificate authorities agreed, through CA/Browser Forum ballot SC-081v3, to cut the maximum lifetime of those certificates in three steps: 200 days from 15 March 2026, 100 days from 15 March 2027 and 47 days from 15 March 2029. The domain checks behind each certificate expire faster too.

One renewal a year was manageable by hand. Eight a year per certificate is not — one missed date means a browser warning, a broken app or a halted payment page. Without automation

Three problems that grow with every cut in lifetime.

None of these is new. Shorter certificates just make each one happen more often.

01 · Human error at scale

One missed date is enough

Renewing by hand means someone remembers, generates a CSR, proves the domain, installs the chain and reloads — on every server. Do that eight times a year per certificate and a slip is a matter of when, not if.

  • Reminders owned by one person
  • Wrong or missing intermediate chain
  • Renewed on one node, not the other
02 · Operational overload

A quarterly chore becomes weekly

With hundreds of certificates, renewals stop being an occasional task and become a standing queue. Engineers spend their weeks on tickets and change windows instead of the projects you hired them for.

  • Ticket queues that never clear
  • Change windows for routine swaps
  • Knowledge stuck with a few people
03 · Security & compliance risk

Gaps show up in audits — or incidents

Expired or misconfigured certificates cause outages, open the door to interception and leave holes auditors find. Shadow certificates nobody tracks are the ones that expire first.

  • Unknown certificates on forgotten hosts
  • Weak keys and old algorithms linger
  • No evidence trail for auditors

What is certificate lifecycle management?

One system that knows every certificate — and renews it before anyone has to.

Certificate lifecycle management (CLM) is the practice, and the software, for keeping track of every certificate an organisation uses and handling each stage automatically: finding it, issuing it to policy, installing it, renewing it on time and revoking it when needed. Lost trust and conversions

Shoppers and app users leave at the first warning page; many don't come back. Card and UPI flows that call your APIs fail outright.

RBI & SEBI expectations

Banks, NBFCs and SEBI-regulated entities are expected to manage availability and IT risk under their frameworks — an avoidable outage is an audit finding.

CERT-In 6-hour reporting

CERT-In's 2022 directions require listed cyber incidents to be reported within 6 hours. A lapsed certificate that leads to interception or a breach can become one.

DPDP Act exposure

If weak or expired TLS exposes personal data, breach intimation duties apply — and the Act allows penalties of up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards.

How an ACME renewal runs

Order, challenge, finalize, install — no human in the loop.

ACME (RFC 8555) is the open protocol that lets a client on your server talk to the certificate authority directly. SCM provides the ACME endpoint; the client handles the rest on schedule.

  1. OrderClient asks SCM's ACME endpoint for a certificate for shop.yourbrand.in
  2. ChallengeCA asks for proof of domain control — an HTTP-01 file or DNS-01 TXT record the client publishes
  3. FinalizeClient sends the CSR; the CA validates and issues the certificate
  4. InstallClient installs the certificate and chain, reloads the server and confirms

Crypto agility & post-quantum

Short lifetimes make algorithm changes routine, not a project.

Quantum computers capable of breaking today's RSA and ECC keys don't exist yet, but standards bodies are preparing — NIST published its first post-quantum standards (ML-KEM and ML-DSA) in 2024. When browsers and CAs adopt them for public TLS, organisations that already replace every certificate automatically every few weeks will switch by changing a policy. Everyone else will be hunting for certificates again.

  • Change key type or CA in policy, roll out on the next renewal
  • Inventory shows which systems still use RSA-2048 or SHA-1-era settings
  • Same automation covers public, private and hybrid certificates
