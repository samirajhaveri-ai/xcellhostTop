    Overview

    Sign-in security decides who gets in. Governance decides what they should still have.

    Access builds up quietly. New hires wait days for accounts, people who change roles keep their old permissions, and ex-employees and contractors stay live in SaaS and SAP long after they leave. Identity Governance & Administration (IGA) is the discipline that fixes this — granting access by rule, reviewing it on a schedule and removing it the moment it is no longer needed.

    XcellHost delivers IGA on Microsoft Entra ID Governance, an add-on to Entra ID P1 or P2 you may already own through Microsoft 365. We connect your HR system, build the lifecycle workflows and access packages, run the first review campaigns with your managers and hand over evidence your auditor can use.

    Why it matters in India

    Most identity incidents start with access nobody remembered to remove.

    Attackers rarely need to break in when a dormant account, an over-privileged role or an ex-vendor login is still active. Governance closes those doors on a schedule — and records that you did.

    Orphaned accounts

    Leavers and ex-contractors still able to sign in to SaaS, VPN or ERP.

    Privilege creep

    People who moved teams and kept every permission they ever had.

    Toxic combinations

    One person able to both raise and approve a payment.

    Spreadsheet reviews

    Certifications that run late, rubber-stamp everything and leave no clean evidence.

    What Indian rules expect · IGA helps you meet them
    0CERT-In incident reporting window — disabling a compromised or orphaned account fast matters
    0CERT-In log retention — export governance and audit logs to your SIEM
    DPDPDigital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — access to personal data limited to those who need it
    RBI · SEBI · IRDAISector frameworks expect periodic user-access reviews and control of privileged access

    IGA supports these requirements; it does not make you compliant on its own. XcellHost maps controls to your regulator's circulars during discovery.
