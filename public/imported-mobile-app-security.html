    One platform · six modules

    Security that ships inside your app, and fraud control that reads the session.

    Fraud on mobile rarely breaks your servers. It works on the phone: a modified app, a hijacked SIM, a stranger watching the screen. Each module below closes one of those doors. Pick what your app needs; all six share one SDK and one console.

    In-app protection, check by check

    Checks that run on every launch, and every screen that matters.

    Runtime self-protection treats the phone as untrusted ground. When a check fails, your policy decides what happens: warn the user, switch off one feature, end the session or block access, and send the signal to your back end.

    Device & SIM binding

    Don't take a login on trust. Check the phone behind it.

    Most mobile fraud starts with someone else holding the account: a swapped SIM, a number re-registered on a new phone, a user talked into installing the app on the fraudster's device. RBI's Master Direction on Digital Payment Security Controls asks regulated entities to bind mobile banking apps to the device. Binding makes the phone itself a factor.

    1. 01RegisterThe phone number is confirmed from the device it is used on, through a verification loop between the app, SMS and your server.
    2. 02BindDevice, SIM and number are linked into one identity and a token is issued at each stage.
    3. 03Re-checkThe binding is checked again at launch and before sensitive actions. A changed SIM or device means re-binding, not a silent login.

    Per Protectt.ai, a silent mobile verification option can confirm the user through the mobile network instead of a typed OTP. Ask us about availability with Indian operators.

    StandardsOWASP Mobile Top 10, briefly

    OWASP's Mobile Top 10 (2024) lists the risks that most often hurt mobile apps: M1 improper credential usage, M2 inadequate supply-chain security, M3 insecure authentication and authorisation, M4 insufficient input and output validation, M5 insecure communication, M6 inadequate privacy controls, M7 insufficient binary protections, M8 security misconfiguration, M9 insecure data storage and M10 insufficient cryptography.

    In-app protection and obfuscation address M7 directly, and binding, API protection and encryption support M1, M3, M5 and M10. Code review and testing still matter for the rest.

    ComplianceRBI device binding: what to check

    Ask four questions of your mobile banking app. Is the app tied to one device and SIM at registration? Is the binding re-checked at launch and before payments? Does a SIM or device change force re-binding through a verified channel? Can you show logs of all three to an auditor?

    If any answer is "partly", the assessment in our plans maps the gap and the module that closes it.

    Threat watchBanking malware sold as a service

    Fraud kits for Android are now rented to criminals who do not write code. Once a victim is persuaded to install one, it can abuse permissions to watch the screen or read messages, then act inside banking apps.

    The defence is layered: malware and screen checks inside your app, device and SIM binding so stolen credentials are not enough, and trust scoring that notices when a session stops behaving like the customer.
