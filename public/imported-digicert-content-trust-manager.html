    Overview

    One trust layer for media and documents. Signed at the source, checkable by anyone.

    Synthetic images and cloned voices make it hard to tell what is real. Labels and file metadata help, but they are easily stripped or rewritten.     Why it matters in India

    India now expects provenance for synthetic media. Signed content helps you show yours.

    Content Credentials don't make you compliant on their own — but tamper-evident origin, history and audit trails help you meet several Indian obligations. Take legal advice for your specific case.

    IT Rules amendment 2026

    Synthetic content labelling

    MeitY's amendment to the IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules, notified 10 February 2026 and in force from 20 February 2026, asks platforms that enable AI-generated content to label it and embed metadata or provenance markers that can't be removed. Signed credentials give publishers a standards-based way to carry that disclosure.

    DPDP Act, 2023

    Personal data in media

    Photos and videos often contain people. A record of who captured, edited and published an asset supports purpose limitation and responding to data-principal requests.

    CERT-In directions, 2022

    6-hour reporting · 180-day logs

    If brand media is impersonated or a signing account is misused, signing audit trails help you investigate fast and keep logs for the 180 days CERT-In expects.

    IT Act, 2000 · RBI / SEBI

    Records you can stand behind

    DigiCert signatures are not Indian DSCs under the IT Act — use a CCA-licensed DSC or eSign where the law requires one. For regulated BFSI notices and investor communications, signed originals make doctored copies easy to disprove.
