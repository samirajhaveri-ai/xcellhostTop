Overview

Many domains, one certificate — and your company name on all of them.

Most Indian businesses run more than one website: a .in and a .com, a shop, a customer portal, a mail server, a brand microsite. Buying a separate certificate for each means separate prices, validations and expiry dates. DigiCert Multi-Domain SSL — also called a SAN or UCC certificate — secures them together: one certificate lists every name as a Subject Alternative Name, up to 250 in total.

It is an OV (organisation-validated) certificate, so DigiCert checks your registered business before issuance and writes its name into the certificate and the dynamic DigiCert seal. The price includes four names; you add, change or remove SANs during the term. XcellHost prepares your MCA or GST documents, plans the SAN list with you, installs it on every server and handles each reissue.

    Multi-Domain SSL in India

    Every domain you run is in scope. One certificate keeps them all covered.

    Indian rules don't name a certificate brand, but they expect personal data in transit to be protected and certificates to be managed as assets. A forgotten second domain is the usual gap.

    DPDP Act, 2023

    Data fiduciaries must take reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal-data breaches. Encrypting every login and form — on every domain you collect data on — is the baseline.

    CERT-In directions (2022)

    Cyber incidents, including website compromise and defacement, must be reported within six hours. Fewer certificates to track means fewer expiry and mis-configuration incidents.

    RBI & SEBI frameworks

    RBI's IT governance directions and SEBI's CSCRF expect strong encryption on customer channels and an inventory of critical assets — certificates and their expiry dates included.

    MeitY & government sites

    MeitY's Guidelines for Indian Government Websites (GIGW) expect HTTPS across public portals. PSUs running several department domains can consolidate them under one OV certificate.

    DPDP Act penalty ceiling
    ₹0crore

    Maximum penalty under the DPDP Act's schedule for failing to take reasonable security safeguards against a personal-data breach.

    • Encrypt every domain that collects data
    • Show a verified company name
    • One expiry date to watch
