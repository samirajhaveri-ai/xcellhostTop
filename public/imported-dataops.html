Overview

Most data problems are found by the person reading the report.

A finance dashboard shows yesterday's numbers. A churn model trains on a half-loaded table. A chatbot answers from a policy that was withdrawn last month. None of these are infrastructure outages — the servers are fine. The data is late, wrong, incomplete or not allowed to be there. That is data downtime.

DataOps is the discipline that prevents it. Pipelines are written as code and reviewed. Every change runs through automated tests. Orchestration runs jobs in the right order and retries the safe ones. Monitors watch freshness, volume and schema. Contracts stop producers from breaking consumers. Lineage shows where each column came from and where personal data flows — which is exactly what the DPDP Act will ask you to show.

Latethe table did not refresh by its deadline
Incompletehalf the rows arrived, or none
Wrongnulls, duplicates, impossible values
Brokena column renamed upstream, silently
Not allowedpersonal data kept past its purpose

Five kinds of data downtime. DataOps puts a test, a monitor or a rule in front of each.

DataOps principles

Eight rules we build every pipeline by.

Our working version of the ideas in the DataOps Manifesto (18 principles) and modern data engineering practice — written for teams that have to answer to auditors as well as analysts.

  1. Pipelines are codeSQL, Python and configuration live in Git, with pull requests, review and history. No hand-edited jobs on a server.
  2. Every change is testedUnit tests for logic, data tests for results — not null, unique, accepted values, row counts, referential checks.
  3. CI/CD for dataChanges build in an isolated environment on sample or cloned data, then promote to production automatically when green.
  4. Orchestrate, don't cronDependencies, retries, backfills and SLAs declared in an orchestrator, so a late source never feeds a half-built table.
  5. Observe the data, not just the jobA job can succeed and still load nothing. Monitor freshness, volume, schema and distribution per table.
  6. Agree contracts at the boundaryProducers publish a schema and quality promise; breaking changes are caught in CI before consumers feel them.
  7. Know your lineageEvery column traceable from source to dashboard, model or RAG index — for impact analysis and for privacy.
  8. Privacy by defaultPersonal data tagged at ingestion, masked where it isn't needed, kept in India and erased when its purpose ends.

    India context
    Purpose, retention, residency — written into the pipeline.
    Indian rules turn data engineering choices into legal ones. DataOps makes them repeatable and provable.
    
    
    
      
    11 Aug 2023DPDP Act, 2023 enacted
    
      
    13 Nov 2025DPDP Rules, 2025 notified; Board provisions in force
    
      
    13 Nov 2026Consent Manager provisions apply
    
      
    13 May 2027Notice, consent, security, breach, retention and rights duties apply
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Purpose limitation
    Tag every column with why it exists
    Under the DPDP Act personal data is processed for a specified purpose, and Section 8(7) requires erasure when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is no longer served — unless another law needs it kept. We carry purpose and legal-basis tags in the catalogue and lineage, so a withdrawal reaches every downstream table and index.
    
      
    Retention & erasure
    Retention as scheduled, tested jobs
    Rule 8 sets three-year inactivity erasure for large e-commerce, online gaming and social media platforms, with at least 48 hours' notice before erasure, and Rule 8(3) asks for relevant personal data, traffic data and logs to be kept for at least one year for specified purposes. We implement both as orchestrated jobs with evidence.
    
      
    RBI data localisation
    Payment data stays in India
    RBI's circular of 6 April 2018 requires payment-system data to be stored only in India. Its FAQ allows processing abroad, but the data must be deleted there and brought back to India within one business day or 24 hours of processing, whichever is earlier. We pin storage and pipelines to Indian regions.
    
      
    Cross-border transfer
    Know where every copy goes
    Section 16 lets the Government restrict transfers of personal data to notified countries, and stricter sector rules (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI) still apply. Lineage shows every cross-border copy — including SaaS tools and AI APIs — before a regulator or customer asks.

Orientation only, not legal advice; your organisation remains responsible for compliance.           FAQ
          DataOps, answered.
          
    
          
      
          DataOps is a way of building and running data pipelines with software engineering discipline: code in version control, automated tests, CI/CD, orchestration, monitoring of the data itself, and clear ownership. The aim is data that arrives on time, is correct, and is used only as allowed.
          
      
          DevOps ships application code. DataOps ships code and data together, and data changes every day even when the code doesn't. So DataOps adds data tests, freshness and volume monitors, contracts between teams and lineage on top of the usual CI/CD.
          
      
          Monitoring the data, not only the jobs: freshness (did it update on time), volume (did the expected rows arrive), schema (did columns change), distribution (are values in normal ranges) and lineage (what is upstream and downstream). A job can succeed and still load bad data; observability catches that.
          
      
          A versioned agreement, usually a YAML file, between the team that produces a dataset and the teams that use it. It lists columns, types, owners, quality rules and personal-data tags. CI checks every change against it, so a dropped or retyped column is caught before it breaks a report or model.
          
      
          You need an orchestrator; which one depends on what you already run. We operate leading open-source orchestrators such as Airflow* and Dagster* and cloud-native schedulers. For a fresh start we usually recommend an open-source option to avoid lock-in.
          
      
          Purpose and personal-data tags in the catalogue, lineage to every copy, masking where data isn't needed and retention as scheduled, tested erasure jobs. That makes Section 8(7) erasure and the Rule 8 retention periods provable. The DPDP Rules' core duties apply from 13 May 2027; compliance stays with you.
          
      
          Under RBI's April 2018 circular, payment-system data must be stored only in India. If it is processed abroad it must be deleted there and brought back within one business day or 24 hours. We pin storage and pipelines to Indian regions and show this in lineage.
          
      
          Yes. Document ingestion, masking, chunking, embedding and index refresh run as orchestrated, monitored pipelines, with retrieval evaluation in CI and withdrawn or personal content removed from the index. It pairs with our RAG, Enterprise RAG and vector database services.
