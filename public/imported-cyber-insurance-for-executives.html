      FAQ

      Executive cyber risk, answered plainly.

      No. XcellHost is not an insurer or an IRDAI-registered insurance intermediary. We provide security services that reduce an executive's personal exposure and help after an incident. Any personal cyber, family cyber or D&O policy is arranged by an IRDAI-registered intermediary partner* or directly with an insurer.

      Founders, CXOs, directors, board members, finance heads and anyone whose voice or name can move money or data, as well as leaders with a public profile. Their families are often targeted too, which is why Protect Plus includes them.

      Personal cyber wordings in India commonly list theft of funds, identity theft, cyber extortion, cyber stalking or bullying, malware and data restoration, privacy and media liability, and legal or counselling costs; some add family members. Reputation or PR support appears in some products. Limits, conditions and exclusions are set by each policy wording.

      Directors' and officers' liability insurance responds to claims against leaders for decisions made in their role, which can include claims after a company cyber incident, depending on the wording. Personal cyber cover deals with losses to you as an individual. A licensed intermediary can explain how the two fit for your situation.

      Common exclusions include known incidents before the policy started, intentional or criminal acts by the insured, losses a bank or wallet provider must reimburse, and in some wordings failures to take basic precautions. Read the exclusions section of the wording before relying on any cover.

      With process, not detection alone. We help you set a written rule that no transfer happens on a voice or video instruction alone, a call-back on a known number, and a family safe word. Detection tools miss good fakes; a call-back to the real you does not.

      Treat it as a possible SIM swap. From another phone, call your carrier to block the SIM, call your bank to freeze transactions, and report any fraud to 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in. Then move one-time passwords to an authenticator app where your bank and email allow it.

      Under RBI's 2017 rules, still in force for transactions until 31 December 2026, a customer has zero liability for a third-party breach reported within three working days. RBI's amendment directions of 24 June 2026 change this for transactions from 1 January 2027: report within five calendar days, and individuals reporting to the bank and to 1930 or the cybercrime portal within five days may get one-time compensation of 85% of a loss up to ₹50,000, capped at ₹25,000.
