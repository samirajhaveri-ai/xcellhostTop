Overview

Encryption is three locks, not one checkbox.

Most mail is already encrypted somewhere. The question is where it stops being encrypted — and who can read it there.

Email crosses at least three places where it can be read: the wire between mail servers, the message sitting in mailboxes, archives and forwards, and the key that unlocks it. TLS protects only the first, and only if both servers agree to use it. Message encryption — S/MIME, Microsoft Purview Message Encryption, Gmail's encryption or a secure portal — protects the content wherever it travels. Key management decides who can unlock it, including your provider.

XcellHost picks the right lock for each kind of mail and sets it up on the platform you already run — Microsoft 365, Google Workspace or any SMTP server. Policies decide what gets encrypted, customers on Gmail or Yahoo can still read what you send, and keys can live in an HSM partition in Mumbai. Licences, certificates and service come on one INR invoice with GST.

    How it works

    One outbound message, four decisions.

    Every message leaving your organisation passes the same checkpoints. Policies make the decisions, so people don't have to.

    1Classify

    DLP or mail-flow rules look for PAN, Aadhaar, account numbers, sensitivity labels, keywords like "confidential" and recipient domains.

    Purview DLP · Gmail DLP · Vortex SEG DLP
    2Choose the lock

    Internal or a partner with S/MIME? Sign and encrypt. A customer on Gmail? Send through the portal. Everyone else? Enforced TLS at minimum.

    Policy decides, not the sender
    3Deliver securely

    The connection uses TLS 1.2 or later; to domains that publish MTA-STS or that you list, delivery fails rather than drop to plain text.

    MTA-STS · DANE · forced TLS
    4Prove it

    Who sent what, encrypted how, opened when — logged for audits, with revocation and expiry where the method supports it.

    Audit log · TLS-RPT reports
    Opportunistic TLS · the default

    Encrypt if the other side agrees.

    Exchange Online and Gmail both try TLS on every connection by default — and both still send the message unencrypted if the receiving server doesn't support it. Microsoft and Google say so in their documentation.

    India compliance

    Encryption helps you meet what Indian law and regulators expect.

    Encryption is a safeguard, not a certificate of compliance. Here is where it fits.

    DPDPDPDP Act 2023 & DPDP Rules 2025

    The Act requires reasonable security safeguards against personal-data breaches. The Rules, notified on 13 November 2025, list encryption, obfuscation, masking or tokenisation among those safeguards, along with access control and logs kept for at least a year. Most of these obligations apply 18 months after notification.

    13 Nov 2025Rules notified+18 monthssecurity rule in force72 hdetailed breach report to the Board
    RBIRegulated entities

    RBI's IT governance and cyber-security directions expect customer data protected in storage and in transit. Encrypted statements and enforced TLS to partners are evidence you can show.

    SEBICSCRF (August 2024)

    The Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework covers data protection and encryption for market intermediaries, scaled by category.

    CERT-InDirections of April 2022

    Incidents reported within 6 hours and ICT logs kept for 180 days in India — including the mail and encryption logs we retain for you.

    KeysKey location

    Regulators increasingly ask where keys live. A Cloud HSM partition in Mumbai gives you a named Indian facility and your own quorum.

    For orientation only, checked October 2026 — not legal advice. Your organisation stays responsible for compliance; confirm current requirements with your counsel, RBI, SEBI, CERT-In or the Data Protection Board.
