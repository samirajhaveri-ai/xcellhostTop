Overview

Your security perimeter now includes everyone you pay.

Most Indian businesses run on dozens of outside providers — software, hosting, outsourced support, payments, logistics, consultants. Each one is given some of your data, some access to your systems, or a part of a service your customers rely on. When their security slips, the breach, the downtime and the regulator's questions still land on you.

XcellHost Third Party Risk Management (TPRM) is a managed programme. We build your vendor register, tier every vendor by the risk it carries, send the right questionnaire, review the evidence that comes back, score and decide, write the contract clauses that matter, and keep watching between reviews. Your team approves; we do the chasing.

Why it matters in India

Outsource the work — never the accountability.

Indian law and regulators are clear: the business that collects the data or holds the licence answers for what its vendors do with it.

01

You stay the data fiduciary

Under the DPDP Act 2023, a processor acts on your behalf under a contract — but the duty to protect personal data and report a breach stays with you.

valid contract · safeguards · breach notice
02

Six hours is not much time

CERT-In expects specified incidents reported within 6 hours. If your vendor finds the incident, your clock depends on how fast they tell you.

notification clause · named contacts
03

Regulators ask for the file

RBI's IT outsourcing directions and SEBI's CSCRF expect documented due diligence, audit rights, sub-contractor oversight and exit plans for material vendors.

due diligence · audit rights · exit
04

Their suppliers are your suppliers

Your call-centre's cloud host and your payroll provider's email system are fourth parties. One shared outage can stop several vendors at once.

sub-processors · concentration

A typical pattern (illustrative): a marketing agency is given a customer export for one campaign. The file sits in a shared folder long after the campaign ends. Nobody asked how it would be deleted. That is a vendor-risk failure, not a firewall failure.

Scope · what we assess

Ten risk domains, scaled to each vendor's tier.

A Tier 1 vendor is assessed across all ten with evidence. A Tier 4 vendor gets a short screen. Pick a tier to see what's in scope.

Security governance

Policies, ownership, ISO 27001 / SOC 2 status and how often they are reviewed.

evidence: certificate, scope statement
Access & identity

MFA, joiner-mover-leaver, privileged access and who on their side can reach your data.

evidence: access review record
Data protection & privacy

Where your data lives, encryption, retention and deletion — and DPDP processor duties.

evidence: data-flow, retention policy
Incident response & notice

How fast they detect, who they tell, and whether that fits your CERT-In and DPDP timelines.

evidence: IR plan, last test date
Business continuity & DR

Recovery targets for the service you buy, tested backups and alternate sites.

evidence: BCP test report
Sub-processors · fourth parties

Who they rely on to serve you, where, and whether they need your consent first.

evidence: sub-processor list
Product & application security

Secure development, vulnerability management and independent testing of what you use.

evidence: test summary, patch SLA
Infrastructure & hosting

Hosting location, network security, logging and log retention for your environment.

evidence: hosting attestation
Regulatory & contract fit

Audit rights, data-localisation terms, RBI / SEBI / IRDAI clauses where they apply.

evidence: contract extract
Financial health & exit

Dependency, substitutability, data return and how you would leave if you had to.

evidence: exit plan

    Methodology

    Seven gates every vendor passes — then the cycle repeats.

    A vendor isn't "approved" once and forgotten. Tier decides how deep each gate goes and how soon the vendor comes round again.

    V-012
    Identify
    Tier
    Assess
    Validate
    Decide
    Contract
    Monitor
    reassess on schedule · or sooner when monitoring raises an alert
    1. 01Identify & inventory

      Vendor list built from your AP ledger, contracts and IT systems — including the ones procurement never saw.

    2. 02Tier by inherent risk

      Data, access, criticality, substitutability and regulatory flags decide Tier 1 to 4.

    3. 03Assess

      The right questionnaire for the tier and type, sent through a vendor portal, chased by us.

    4. 04Validate evidence

      Analysts read the certificates, policies and test reports. Tier 1 adds a remote validation call.

    5. 05Score & decide

      Residual risk per domain, a recommendation — approve, approve with conditions, or escalate — and your sign-off.

    6. 06Contract & remediate

      Clauses for notice, audit, sub-processors and exit; agreed fixes tracked to closure with the vendor.

    7. 07Monitor & reassess

      External signals watched continuously for critical vendors; scheduled reassessment by tier; offboarding with data-return proof.

    8. CycleWhen each tier comes round again
      • Tier 1every 12 months
      • Tier 212–18 months
      • Tier 324 months
      • Tier 4at contract change
