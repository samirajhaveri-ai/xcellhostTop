01 · the basics

IPAM is the system of record for your address space. DDI ties it to DNS and DHCP.

Every server, camera, VPN pool and cloud subnet needs an address that nothing else is using. IPAM tracks which ranges exist, who owns them and which addresses are live. Add DNS and DHCP and you get DDI: an address handed out by DHCP is recorded in IPAM and published in DNS by the same system.

DNS

Names to addresses. A, AAAA and PTR records created from the IPAM record, not typed in a second console.

DHCP

Scopes, reservations and leases. Each lease updates the address record with its MAC, hostname and expiry.

IPAM

Supernets, subnets, VLANs, VRFs and every address: owner, state, last seen, history.

Why the spreadsheet stops working
  • It is out of date the day it is saved. DHCP hands out addresses every minute; a sheet only knows what someone typed.
  • Two copies, two truths. Network, server and cloud teams each keep their own version. Nobody knows which one is right.
  • No conflict check. A sheet will not stop you assigning an address that is already live, or a cloud CIDR that overlaps the office LAN.
  • No audit trail. When an auditor asks who changed a range and when, a sheet has no answer.
  • IPv6 does not fit. A single /64 holds 18.4 quintillion addresses. You track prefixes and assignments, not rows.

VLSM subnet planner. Split a supernet by host count.

Enter a supernet and the hosts each subnet needs. The planner sizes each subnet, packs them largest first on aligned boundaries, and shows what is left. Add an IPv6 prefix to give every subnet its own /64. Nothing leaves your browser.

Sizing: each subnet is the smallest power of two that holds the hosts plus the reserved addresses — 2 on a classic LAN (network and broadcast), 5 on AWS* and Azure*, 4 on GCP*. Minimum sizes: /30 on-prem, /28 on AWS*, /29 on Azure* and GCP*. Subnets are placed largest first so every block starts on its own boundary. IPv6 subnets are always /64 (RFC 4291), counted from the prefix you enter.

07 · compliance

SECURITY & COMPLIANCE

IPAM does not make you compliant on its own. It gives you the asset, time and change evidence that these rules ask for.

CERT-In Directions · 28 April 2022

Time, logs and IP records

  • Clock sync. Systems must sync to the NTP servers of NIC or NPL, or to servers traceable to them. IPAM tracks which NTP source each subnet's DHCP scope hands out.
  • 180-day logs. Logs of ICT systems kept securely for a rolling 180 days, within India. IPAM's DHCP lease and change history is stored in Mumbai and kept at least that long.
  • Who had this IP? When an incident names an address, the lease history answers which device held it at that timestamp — the question you need to answer inside the 6-hour reporting window.
  • Providers' IP allotment records. Data centres, cloud and VPN providers must keep records of IPs allotted to customers. IPAM is where that record lives.

The Directions do not themselves list an asset inventory; that requirement comes from ISO 27001, below.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 · Annex A 5.9

Inventory of assets

  • Inventory with owners. A.5.9 asks for an inventory of information and associated assets, with owners. Every subnet and address in IPAM carries an owner and a purpose.
  • Kept current. Discovery flags devices that appear without a record, so the inventory does not drift.
  • Change evidence. The audit log shows who assigned or released what, and when.
DPDP Act 2023 · where it applies

Personal data in the record

  • Device-to-person links. A lease that ties an IP and MAC to an employee's laptop can be personal data. Limit who can see user fields with RBAC.
  • Stored in India. Managed IPAM runs in our Mumbai Tier IV data centre.
  • Retention you set. Keep lease history as long as your log policy needs, then purge.

Summary of CERT-In Directions No. 20(3)/2022-CERT-In dated 28 April 2022, clauses (i), (ii), (iv) and (v), and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A control 5.9. Read the official texts for your obligations; this is not legal advice.
