    Overview

    AI agents now act on your systems. Each one needs an identity you can prove and revoke.

    DigiCert AI Trust Manager is DigiCert's control plane for AI, part of the DigiCert ONE platform, announced as generally available in September 2026. XcellHost licenses it for Indian businesses and runs it for you.

    Teams are wiring AI agents into CRMs, ticket queues, code repositories and payment flows faster than security can list them. Most of those agents borrow a person's login or a shared API key, so nobody can say which agent did what, on whose authority, or how to stop it. Per DigiCert, AI Trust Manager discovers the agents, models and MCP servers you already run, keeps a live inventory, and gives each agent a cryptographically verifiable identity with defined permissions.

    The approach borrows from public key infrastructure, the same model that lets any browser check a website certificate without knowing the site in advance. DigiCert calls the agent credential an AI Passport; policy "visas" say which systems, data and actions it may use, and for how long. XcellHost assesses your AI estate, onboards the platform, connects it to your identity and DevOps stack, and watches it round the clock — with one GST invoice in rupees.

    Identity that travels

    The passport is a portable, signed credential. Another system — or another company — can verify it independently, per DigiCert.

    Visas with an expiry

    Each visa names a system, the data or actions allowed and how long the authority lasts. Standing access becomes time-boxed access.

    A human answers for it

    Every agent is tied to an owner — whether you built it, bought it or inherited it — so audit questions have a name attached.

    Kill switch at the source

    When trust changes, credentials are updated, permissions expire, or one agent or a whole group is revoked and quarantined under owner or issuer policy.

    Why it matters in India

    Regulators ask who did what with personal data. "An AI agent" is not an answer.

    No Indian rule names AI passports yet. But the DPDP Act, CERT-In directions and sector regulators all expect accountability, logs and fast incident response — and ungoverned agents break all three. AI Trust Manager helps you meet those expectations; it does not make you compliant on its own.

    DPDP Act 2023Accountability for processing

    A data fiduciary answers for processing done on its behalf, including by software, and must keep reasonable security safeguards. Penalties reach ₹250 crore. Owner-linked agent identities and scoped visas show who allowed an agent near personal data.

    CERT-In 20226-hour reporting · 180-day logs

    Specified incidents must be reported within six hours and ICT logs kept for 180 days in India. Session records and revocation events for each agent make scoping faster.

    RBI · SEBI · IRDAISector expectations

    RBI's IT governance directions, SEBI's CSCRF and IRDAI's cyber guidelines expect asset inventories, access control and audit trails. AI agents in a bank, broker or insurer are assets and identities like any other.

    IT Act 2000Reasonable security practices

    Section 43A ties liability to reasonable security practices for sensitive data. Signed identities and integrity checks are evidence that controls exist and work.
