Compliance note

XcellHost Cloud Services Pvt. Ltd. is not an insurer and is not registered with IRDAI as an insurance intermediary. We do not solicit, sell, underwrite, price or service insurance, and nothing on this page is insurance advice. Under the Insurance Act, 1938 (sections 42 and 42D, as amended), only insurers, licensed agents and IRDAI-registered insurance intermediaries — such as insurance brokers and corporate agents — may solicit or arrange insurance. Where you choose to buy cover, it is arranged by an IRDAI-registered intermediary partner* or directly with an insurer.

Insurance is the subject matter of solicitation. Cover is subject to the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy wording issued by the insurer; read the policy wording and prospectus before you buy. No premium, limit, claim outcome or saving is promised here. You can check any intermediary's registration on IRDAI's list of registered insurance entities at policyholder.gov.in.

Ransomware & extortion

Encrypted servers, stolen files and a deadline. The cost is the outage, the forensics and the restore, long before any demand.

Business email compromise

A look-alike domain or a hijacked mailbox changes the bank details on a real invoice. The money leaves through your own process.

Personal-data breaches

Customer or employee data exposed means notices, a Board intimation under the DPDP Act and questions from the people affected.

Supplier & cloud outages

An incident at a provider stops your billing or orders. Whether that is covered depends on the wording, so it needs reading early.

        Cyber cover for businesses, answered plainly.

        No. XcellHost is not an insurer and is not an IRDAI-registered insurance intermediary. We do security work that makes a business easier to insure and better prepared for a claim: readiness assessments, managed controls, an incident-response retainer and evidence packs. Insurance itself is arranged by an IRDAI-registered intermediary partner* or directly with an insurer.

        Insurers, their licensed agents and IRDAI-registered insurance intermediaries such as insurance brokers and corporate agents, under the Insurance Act, 1938 and IRDAI's intermediary regulations. You can check a firm's registration on IRDAI's list of registered insurance entities at policyholder.gov.in before you deal with it.

        Commercial wordings commonly list first-party costs — incident response and forensics, data restoration, business interruption, cyber extortion, notification and reputation costs, and sometimes funds lost to cyber crime — and third-party liability for privacy and security failures, data-protection investigations and digital media content. Limits, sub-limits and exclusions differ, and the policy wording issued to you decides.

        Many wordings include a cyber extortion section, but terms vary. Insurers usually require you to notify them quickly and to get their written consent before engaging vendors or paying anything, and some limit or exclude certain losses. Backups you can restore from are what keep a ransomware event short, whatever the policy says.

        Some wordings include data-protection fines and penalties only where they are insurable by law, and exclude loss that is uninsurable. Whether penalties imposed by the Data Protection Board under the DPDP Act 2023 can be insured is subject to law and the wording, so treat it as uncertain and focus on not incurring them.

        Most proposal forms ask about multi-factor authentication, endpoint detection and response, backups kept offline or immutable, patching timelines, email security, privileged access, staff training, an incident-response plan, logging and how you handle third parties and personal data. Our assessment covers all twelve and gives you evidence for each.

        We cannot promise that. Premiums, terms and acceptance are decided by insurers. Stronger, evidenced controls give an intermediary a better story to take to the market and reduce the chance of an incident in the first place; what any insurer does with that is its own decision.

        CERT-In's directions of 28 April 2022, issued under section 70B(6) of the IT Act, require service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations to report specified cyber incidents within 6 hours of noticing them, and to keep logs for 180 days within India. Our retainer drafts and files that report with you.

        Some cyber wordings have a cyber crime or social-engineering section, often with a lower sub-limit and conditions such as call-back verification of bank-detail changes; others leave it to a separate crime policy. Report the loss to your bank and to 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in at once, because funds can sometimes be held if you act fast.
