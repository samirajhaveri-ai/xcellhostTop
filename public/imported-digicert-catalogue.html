Why DigiCert

SSL made simple. PKI done right.

What sets DigiCert certificates apart, checked against DigiCert's own pages on 11 November 2026.

DigiCert is a US certificate authority founded in 2003 and based in Lehi, Utah. In 2017 it bought Symantec's website security and PKI business, which brought the GeoTrust, Thawte and RapidSSL brands under the same roots. The DigiCert-branded certificates on this page are all OV or EV: DigiCert checks that your organisation legally exists before it issues, and puts the verified company name in the certificate.

Banks, payment pages, hospitals and government portals pick DigiCert when the name in the certificate has to stand up to scrutiny. XcellHost supplies every edition below in rupees, prepares your MCA, GST and authorisation documents for validation, installs the certificate on your servers and reissues it through your subscription. If you only need domain validation, GeoTrust and RapidSSL are DigiCert brands that issue DV certificates for less.

Validation process & timelines

What DigiCert checks, and how long it usually takes.

DigiCert verifies domain control and legal existence for OV, and adds operational existence and approval by a verified representative for EV. Timings are typical, from our product pages; DigiCert doesn't publish guaranteed times.

  1. Day 0Order & CSR

    We generate the CSR with you (RSA 2048+ or ECC P-256) and place the order.

  2. MinutesDomain control

    DNS TXT or CNAME record, approval email, an HTTP file, or ACME HTTP-01 / DNS-01.

  3. Day 1–3Organisation check

    Legal existence against public records: MCA (CIN/LLPIN), GST or a registration certificate.

  4. MinutesIssue & install

    Once validated, DigiCert issues; we install, test the chain and turn on HTTPS redirects.

Prevalidate once, issue in minutes. DigiCert reuses organisation validation for up to 397 days, so later orders and reissues for the same company skip the paperwork. Standard SSL is listed as issued in minutes after validation; Secure Site and Pro as 1–3 business days the first time.

  1. Day 0Order & EV forms

    CSR plus the EV request, signed by a director or authorised signatory.

  2. MinutesDomain control

    Same DCV methods as OV, for every name on the certificate.

  3. Day 1–3Legal & operational existence

    Registration, active status and a physical presence, checked against government and third-party sources.

  4. Day 2–5Verified call & approval

    A call to a publicly listed number and approval by a verified representative of the organisation.

  5. HoursIssue & install

    Issued once vetting clears. We install, test and add the seal if your edition includes one.

EV typically takes 1–5 business days the first time. Most delays are a phone number that isn't in a public directory or a mismatch between the MCA name and the CSR. We check both before ordering. Secure Site editions get priority validation.

Why automation now · CA/B Forum Ballot SC-081

Public TLS certificates get shorter every year until 2029.

Ballot SC-081v3, passed by the CA/Browser Forum in April 2025, cuts the maximum validity of publicly trusted TLS certificates in three steps. Domain validation reuse shrinks with it.

Before 15 Mar 2026398days maximumDCV reuse 398 days
From 15 Mar 2026200days maximumDCV reuse 200 days · in force now
From 15 Mar 2027100days maximumDCV reuse 100 days
From 15 Mar 202947days maximumDCV reuse 10 days
About 8 reissues a year at 47 days

Each certificate is replaced every six to seven weeks. Done by hand across 20 sites, that is more than 150 installs a year.

ACME in CertCentral

DigiCert's CertCentral issues ACME credentials (a directory URL with a key ID and HMAC key) for third-party clients such as Certbot. For OV and EV, the organisation must be prevalidated and automatic approval turned on.

DigiCert is slightly stricter

DigiCert's documentation limits domain validation reuse for OV and EV to 199 days from 24 February 2026, a day inside the 200-day rule. Plan renewals with a buffer.
