    Why AI programmes stall

    Most AI efforts don't fail on the model. They stall between the demo and the business.

    These are the patterns we see when organisations move from trying AI tools to running AI in production. Each one maps to a stage and a gate in the framework.

    Pilots with no business owner

    A team builds a clever proof of concept, but nobody owns the outcome or the budget to take it further.

    Stage 1Named sponsor and value hypothesis before any build
    Data that isn't ready

    Documents are scattered, access rights are unclear and nobody knows which data may legally go into an AI system.

    Stage 2Data readiness scored and gaps owned
    Shadow AI and no policy

    Staff paste customer data into public chatbots because no approved tool or acceptable-use rule exists.

    Stage 3AI policy, approved-tools list and risk register
    Success never defined

    The pilot "went well", but there's no baseline, metric or threshold to justify scaling it.

    Stage 4Success metrics and evaluation set agreed up front
    Tools bought, habits unchanged

    Licences are assigned but usage drops after a month because workflows, training and support didn't change.

    Stage 5Change management, champions and role-based training
    Costs and quality drift quietly

    Token bills grow, answers degrade after a model update, and nobody is watching either.

    Stage 6Monitoring, drift checks and FinOps for tokens

            Six pillars

            Every stage is checked against the same six pillars, so nothing is left for later.

            The pillars are how we score readiness, plan work and report progress. They run across all six stages rather than happening once.

            P1

            Strategy & Value

            AI tied to business goals, with a sponsor, a portfolio and a value case per use case.

            • AI vision and principles
            • Ranked use-case portfolio
            • Value hypothesis and KPIs
            P2

            People & Culture

            Leaders, staff and specialists with the skills, incentives and confidence to use AI well.

            • AI literacy by role
            • Champions network
            • Change and comms plan
            P3

            Data

            Data that is findable, of known quality, classified and lawful to use for each AI purpose.

            • Source inventory and owners
            • Classification and lineage
            • Access and DPDP purpose checks
            P4

            Platform & Technology

            A shared, secure platform for models, retrieval, agents and integration instead of one-off stacks.

            • Landing zone and model access
            • LLMOps / MLOps pipelines
            • Integration with core systems
            P5

            Governance, Risk & Ethics

            Clear accountability, risk and impact assessment, and human oversight proportional to the stakes.

            • AI policy and review board
            • Risk and impact assessments
            • Human-in-the-loop rules
            P6

            Security

            AI-specific threats covered: prompt injection, data leakage, agent permissions and third-party models.

            • Guardrails and DLP for AI
            • Identity for agents and apps
            • Logging and SOC monitoring

            MicrosoftCloud Adoption Framework — AI

            Adoption phases shown as Plan, Ready, Govern, Secure and Manage. Guidance on learn.microsoft.com (updated October 2026).

            Google CloudAI Adoption Framework

            Four pillars (people, process, technology, data), six themes (Lead, Learn, Access, Scale, Secure, Automate) and three phases: tactical, strategic, transformational. Published 2020.

            AWSCAF for AI, ML & generative AI

            Whitepaper of 13 February 2024 built on the six AWS CAF perspectives (business, people, governance, platform, security, operations); adds a "responsible use of AI" capability. AWS now marks it for historical reference.

            NISTAI RMF 1.0 + GenAI Profile

            AI RMF 1.0 released 26 January 2023 with four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Generative AI Profile, NIST AI 600-1, released 26 July 2024. NIST says the AI RMF is being revised.

            ISO / IECISO/IEC 42001 & 23894

            ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (published December 2023) specifies an AI management system with Annex A controls. ISO/IEC 23894:2023 (February 2023) gives guidance on AI risk management.

            OECDOECD AI Principles

            Adopted May 2019, revised November 2023 and May 2024. Five values-based principles, from inclusive growth to accountability, and five recommendations to governments.

            Responsible AI & India compliance

            Built for India's rules, ready for your export markets.

            The framework produces the evidence these instruments ask for. It helps you meet your obligations; legal interpretation and accountability stay with your organisation and counsel. Dates verified October 2026.

            MeitY · Nov 2025India AI Governance Guidelines

            Unveiled 5 November 2025 under the IndiaAI Mission: seven guiding sutras and recommendations across six pillars, relying on existing laws where possible rather than a new AI act.

            Stages 1, 3 · AI principles, accountability, risk classification
            IndiaAI MissionIndiaAI Mission

            Approved by the Union Cabinet in March 2024 with ₹10,371.92 crore over five years. Its seven pillars include IndiaAI Compute, AIKosh datasets and Safe & Trusted AI.

            Stage 5 · sovereign compute and Indian datasets as options
            DPDP Act 2023 · Rules 2025Digital Personal Data Protection

            Rules notified November 2025 with phased commencement: Consent Manager provisions from November 2026, most obligations from May 2027. Breaches go to the Board without delay, with a detailed report within 72 hours.

            Stages 2, 3 · purpose, notice, consent and breach readiness for AI data
            CERT-In · 28 Apr 2022CERT-In directions

            Reportable cyber incidents to CERT-In within 6 hours; ICT system logs kept for 180 days within India. AI apps, agents and their logs are in scope like any other system.

            Stages 3, 6 · logging, incident runbooks, SOC
            RBI · 13 Aug 2025RBI FREE-AI framework

            Committee report on Responsible and Ethical Enablement of AI: 7 sutras and 26 recommendations across innovation enablement and risk mitigation, for regulated entities.

            Stages 3–6 · board policy, inventory, assurance, monitoring
            SEBISEBI AI/ML expectations

            A February 2025 amendment makes intermediaries responsible for the AI tools they use; a June 2025 consultation paper proposed guidelines for responsible AI/ML use in securities markets. We confirm the current status during scoping.

            Stages 3, 4 · model governance, testing, investor protection
            EU AI Act — for Indian exporters

            Applies if you place AI systems on the EU market or their output is used in the EU. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended by the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (in force 27 July 2026).

            1. 1 Aug 2024Act enters into force
            2. 2 Feb 2025Prohibited practices and AI literacy
            3. 2 Aug 2025General-purpose AI model obligations
            4. 2 Aug 2026General application, incl. most transparency duties
            5. 2 Dec 2026Marking of AI-generated content for systems already on the market
            6. 2 Dec 2027High-risk systems in Annex III (stand-alone)
            7. 2 Aug 2028High-risk AI in Annex I products
