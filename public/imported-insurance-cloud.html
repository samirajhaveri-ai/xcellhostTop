What Insurance Cloud is

A cloud for policy, claims and customer data — built around IRDAI's questions.

Insurers and intermediaries can run on cloud, but they stay accountable for it. IRDAI's Information & Cyber Security Guidelines (2023, revised April 2026) look for an empanelled cloud provider, tested DR, regular external testing and proper data erasure at exit. The outsourcing chapter of the IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests, Operations and Allied Matters of Insurers) Regulations, 2024 asks for a board-approved policy, confidentiality, and IRDAI's right to inspect the provider's books, records and systems.

XcellCloud Insurance Cloud answers that list with India-hosted infrastructure in our Mumbai Tier IV data centre, DR at a partner data centre in India*, and managed security from the same team: 24×7 SOC, WAF and bot defence for portals and apps, PAM, Cloud HSM and immutable backups — plus a due-diligence pack for your outsourcing committee.

Industry challenges

The insurance IT risk register — and the response to each line.

Typical issues insurers and intermediaries raise with us. Ratings are illustrative, not survey results.

  1. R1
    Claims backlogs at peak

    Monsoon, motor-accident spikes or a regional catastrophe multiply claim intake overnight; on-prem servers queue them.

    High

    Capacity added in hours on the Mumbai cloud; document storage that grows without new hardware.

  2. R2
    Fraud hiding across claims

    Same phone, bank account, garage or hospital across many claims — invisible when data sits in silos.

    High

    Fraud analytics and link analysis on data held in India; GPU on quote for heavier models.

  3. R3
    Outsourcing scrutiny

    The board's outsourcing committee needs due diligence, confidentiality terms and IRDAI inspection rights in every material contract.

    Medium

    Due-diligence pack, inspection clause and exit plan ready before you sign.

  4. R4
    Portals and apps under attack

    Credential stuffing on customer logins, bots scraping quotes, API abuse on aggregator integrations.

    High

    WAAP with bot and API protection in front of every portal, watched by the SOC.

  5. R5
    DR that has never been tested

    A DR site exists on paper; nobody has switched policy admin over and timed it.

    Medium

    Managed DR with yearly drills (at least), timings recorded and signed.

  6. R6
    Personal and health data everywhere

    Proposal forms, medical reports and KYC copies in shared drives, email and vendor systems.

    High

    Encrypted document stores, access logging and DPO support for DPDP duties.
