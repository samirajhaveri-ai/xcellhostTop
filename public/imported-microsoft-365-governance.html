The five layers

What good looks like, layer by layer.

Read it bottom-up for risk and top-down for users. Each layer has a job, an owner and evidence you can show an auditor.

Who can get in, and how

Identity & Access

Every person, guest, device and agent signs in through Microsoft Entra ID. Conditional Access decides what each sign-in can reach. Admin rights are eligible, not permanent, and switch on for a few hours with approval in Privileged Identity Management.

  • Phishing-resistant MFA for admins; MFA for everyone
  • Only compliant, Intune-managed devices reach sensitive apps
  • Two cloud-only emergency accounts with passkeys, excluded from blocking policies and tested at least every 90 days
  • Agents get an Entra agent identity and a human sponsor

What people can see and share

Information Protection

Microsoft Purview sensitivity labels classify mail and files; DLP stops personal and financial data leaving by email, chat or link; retention keeps what the law asks and deletes the rest. Before Copilot reads your sites, oversharing reports find content open to everyone.

  • A short label set people actually use (four to five labels)
  • DLP moved from audit to enforce, with policy tips
  • Retention schedule mapped to Indian record-keeping rules
  • External sharing defaults narrowed; guest links expire

Where the work happens

Workloads

Exchange Online, SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams, Power Platform and Microsoft 365 Copilot are where data is created and shared. Governance here is lifecycle: who may create a Team or site, who owns it, when it is archived and which connectors and agents may touch it.

  • At least two owners for every Team and site
  • Inactive sites flagged, attested or archived
  • Power Platform DLP policies by environment
  • Copilot switched on after permissions are cleaned up

How you know, and who acts

Monitoring & Response

Defender XDR joins alerts into incidents, Purview Audit records who did what (180 days in Audit Standard; one year for core workloads in Audit Premium), Secure Score tracks configuration and Microsoft Sentinel keeps the long record. None of it helps unless an alert reaches a named person with a time limit.

  • Every alert type routed to a named responder
  • Audit retention set to your policy, not the default
  • Secure Score actions assigned and reviewed monthly
  • CERT-In reporting step inside six hours

The base layer

Resilience & Recovery

Retention isn't backup. Microsoft 365 Backup or a third-party backup gives restore points outside day-to-day deletion; a backup only counts once a restore has been tested and timed. The incident response plan and emergency access procedure are written, reachable offline and drilled.

  • Exchange, SharePoint and OneDrive backed up; Entra ID configuration too
  • Restore test every six months with the time recorded
  • Incident response plan with names and phone numbers
  • Break-glass procedure printed and stored securely

Ownership & accountability

A control without an owner is a setting waiting to drift.

The tools come with the licence. Names don't. These seven habits turn configuration into governance.

Named owner per layer

One person per layer, written down, with a deputy. Not "IT".

Site & team owners

Two owners for every Team and site, who confirm membership each quarter.

Agent sponsors

Entra lets you set a human sponsor on each agent identity; sponsorship passes to their manager when they leave.

Exception register

Every policy exclusion has a reason, an approver and an expiry date.

Access reviews

Guests, groups and admin roles re-approved on a schedule (Entra ID P2 or Governance).

Alert response owners

Each alert type maps to a responder and a response time.

Risk acceptance by name

When a gap stays open, a named executive signs for it — with a review date.
