    Overview

    Your AI agents log in too. Most of them with keys nobody owns.

    Every chatbot, copilot, RAG pipeline and AI agent reaches your data through a non-human identity — a service account, an API key, an OAuth app, a managed identity or a newer agent identity. They outnumber employees many times over, they rarely use MFA, and they are often created in a hurry, given broad access "to make it work", and then forgotten. When one leaks, the attacker gets everything that identity could touch.

    XcellHost AI Identity Security brings those identities under the same discipline you apply to people. We find them across your clouds, SaaS apps and code, give each one a named owner, cut its permissions to the tasks it actually performs, replace standing secrets with short-lived tokens issued just in time, and review every identity with its owner each quarter. Start with a one-time assessment, or let our SOC run it as a managed service — on a GST invoice in rupees.

    Why it matters

    An agent is only as safe as the identity it runs on.

    Most AI incidents involving data are, underneath, identity failures: a key that leaked, a scope that was too broad, an account nobody switched off. These are the risk categories we assess — described at category level, with what each one costs the business.

    7 of 7 systems reachableIf this key leaks, so does everything it can open.

    Leaked & long-lived secrets

    Keys in code, chat threads, notebooks and agent configs that never expire. Impact: silent data access for months.

    OWASP NHI2 · NHI7

    Over-privileged agents

    Tenant-wide or write scopes granted "to make it work". Impact: one mistake or misuse reaches every system the agent can.

    OWASP NHI5 · ASI03 · LLM06

    No owner, no offboarding

    Identities outlive the project, the vendor or the employee who made them. Impact: orphaned access nobody will notice.

    OWASP NHI1

    Agent goal & tool misuse

    Manipulated inputs push an agent to use its tools outside the task. Impact: the damage is bounded only by its permissions.

    OWASP ASI01 · ASI02 · LLM01

    Third-party & MCP connections

    Plugins, MCP servers and SaaS integrations holding your tokens. Impact: a supplier breach becomes yours.

    OWASP NHI3 · ASI04 · ASI07

    Shared & reused identities

    One key across agents, environments or humans. Impact: no audit trail you can attribute — a DPDP and CERT-In problem.

    DPDP Act 2023 & DPDP Rules 2025

    Rules notified in November 2025, with most duties phased in over 18 months. Reasonable security safeguards include access control and logs; a personal-data breach must be intimated without delay, with a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours. Agent identities touching personal data fall inside this.

    CERT-In directions (April 2022)

    Reportable incidents within 6 hours and ICT logs kept for 180 days in India. Attributable, per-agent identities make both possible.

    RBI FREE-AI (August 2025)

    The RBI committee's Framework for Responsible and Ethical Enablement of AI: 7 sutras and 26 recommendations for regulated entities, including governance, accountability and security of AI systems.

    SEBI AI/ML consultation (June 2025)

    Proposed guidelines on responsible AI/ML use by market participants — accountability, testing, records and controls. Proposed, not yet final; we track the outcome.

    India AI Governance Guidelines (MeitY, Nov 2025)

    Seven principles including accountability and "safety, resilience & sustainability"; a principles-based approach rather than a new law.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Any identity that isn't a person: service accounts, API keys and tokens, OAuth apps, managed identities, workload identities, certificates — and now AI agents. They let software talk to software, and in most companies they far outnumber employees.

      Agents act on their own, call many tools and are created quickly by many teams. Each one needs credentials, and each one can be steered by the content it reads. If its identity is over-privileged, a manipulated or faulty agent can do everything that identity allows.

      The agent gets only the scopes its approved tasks need — read before write, its own mailbox before all mailboxes — and actions with real-world effect, such as payments or deletions, wait for a human to approve. The permission planner above shows the idea.

      Instead of a key that works forever, the agent receives a token when a task starts, scoped to that task and expiring in minutes. If it leaks, it is already useless or soon will be. We design this using your identity provider, cloud workload identity or a vault.
