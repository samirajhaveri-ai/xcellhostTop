What is cloud repatriation?

Cloud repatriation is the process of moving workloads and data out of a public cloud — AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud or Oracle — back to private or dedicated infrastructure. Businesses do it to replace unpredictable usage-based bills and egress fees with fixed costs, to get consistent performance on dedicated hardware, and to keep regulated data in a known jurisdiction. XcellHost repatriates workloads to private cloud, bare metal or hybrid environments in Indian data centres, billed monthly in INR with no egress charges.

Why businesses are reconsidering public cloud

Public cloud is brilliant for getting started and for bursting. For steady, data-heavy workloads, three pressures keep pushing teams to look again.

Costs that keep climbing

Usage-based pricing looked cheap at launch. At scale, egress, storage operations, idle instances and support plans turn into a bill nobody can forecast.

Performance you can't pin down

Virtualised, multi-tenant capacity means latency and throughput vary with other people's workloads — hard to explain to a customer.

Data in the wrong place

Regulators, auditors and enterprise clients increasingly ask exactly where personal and financial data lives, and who can reach it.

What moves well — and what should stay

Often better left on public cloud

  • Workloads built on proprietary managed services
  • Burst-heavy jobs that scale 10× for minutes
  • Short-lived experiments you'll delete next month

Frequently Asked Questions

What is cloud repatriation?
Cloud repatriation is the process of moving workloads and data out of a public cloud — AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud or Oracle — back to private or dedicated infrastructure. Businesses do it to replace unpredictable usage-based bills and egress fees with fixed costs, to get consistent performance on dedicated hardware, and to keep regulated data in a known jurisdiction. XcellHost repatriates workloads to private cloud, bare metal or hybrid environments in Indian data centres, billed monthly in INR with no egress charges.
Why are businesses moving off AWS, Azure and Google Cloud?
Four reasons come up most: bills that grow faster than usage (egress, idle capacity, support plans), inconsistent performance on shared hardware, limited control over the OS and hardware, and stricter requirements on where data is stored and who can access it.
Which workloads should not be repatriated?
Globally distributed front-ends, workloads built deeply on proprietary managed services, very spiky burst jobs and short-lived experiments usually belong on public cloud. We'll say so in the audit rather than move them for the sake of it.
How much downtime does a migration involve?
Data is replicated ahead of time and the cut-over is rehearsed on a copy, so the final switch fits into a planned window — typically a few hours out of business hours — with a tested rollback plan if anything unexpected happens.
Can we keep using Kubernetes and containers?
Yes. Container and Kubernetes workloads move cleanly — usually with the same manifests and Helm charts — onto bare metal or private cloud, which is one of the easiest repatriation paths.
Does repatriation help with the DPDP Act?
It helps you answer data-residency and access questions cleanly: data sits in named Indian data centres with documented access controls and backups, and our certified operations produce the evidence auditors ask for. DPDP compliance still depends on your own processes — we can help with those too.
Is cloud repatriation actually happening?
Yes. Many organisations are moving selected workloads off public cloud — not abandoning it — after finding that steady, data-heavy workloads cost more there than on dedicated infrastructure. The pattern is increasingly hybrid: each workload where it runs best.
How do we know if we're a good candidate?
Start with a workload audit: which systems run 24×7 at steady load, how much you pay for data transfer, how much capacity is idle, and how dependent each workload is on proprietary managed services. The readiness check on this page gives a quick first view; our free audit gives the numbers.
Where do repatriated workloads run?
In XcellHost's Indian data centres, on the target that suits each workload: private cloud, bare-metal servers, Performance Cloud, GPU servers, co-location for your own hardware, or a hybrid design that keeps some services on the hyperscaler.
Are there really no egress fees?
Yes. Bandwidth is included in the plan and we don't charge per GB for data leaving your servers. If your traffic grows well beyond the included allocation, we'll discuss a larger port before any charge appears.
What about the egress bill for leaving the hyperscaler?
The one-time transfer out of your current cloud is charged by that provider. We plan the migration to minimise it — compressing, deduplicating and moving only what's needed — and include the estimate in the TCO so there are no surprises.
What does XcellHost charge for the migration?
The workload audit and TCO comparison are free. Migration effort is quoted up front as a fixed project fee based on scope, and ongoing infrastructure is a fixed monthly price in INR with a GST invoice.
