Why this course

AI writes fluent law. Some of it does not exist.

Models draft a notice or summarise a judgment in seconds, and they can invent a case name, a citation or a ratio with complete confidence. This course teaches a method: what to give AI, what never to paste in, and how to verify output before it reaches a client or a court.

Courts, statutes & AI

What has changed since your last research memo.

The course keeps to the official record. Items below were checked against Supreme Court, India Code, Ministry and PIB sources in October 2026.

  1. 2023
    e-Courts Phase III

    Outlay of ₹7,210 crore, with funds earmarked for future technology including AI. Delivered through the e-Committee of the Supreme Court and the Department of Justice.

  2. 1 Jul 2024
    BNS, BNSS and BSA in force

    The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act. Module 2 includes an old-to-new section mapping drill.

  3. Nov 2025
    DPDP Rules notified

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 came with an 18-month phased compliance timeline. Law firms hold client and employee personal data.

  4. Feb 2026
    SUVAS and SUPACE

    Per a PIB note, SUVAS translates Supreme Court judgments into 18 Indian languages for the e-SCR portal, with human review; SUPACE remains experimental and is not in regular judicial use.

  5. Jun 2026
    Draft AI regulations for courts

    The Supreme Court invited comments on the draft Regulations for Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Courts, 2026. They are a draft, not in force.

  6. 2 Jul 2026
    Zero tolerance for unverified AI precedents

    In Pooja Ramesh Singh v. Jammu and Kashmir Bank Ltd. (2026 INSC 668) the Supreme Court set aside orders that relied on fake or misquoted judgments, said citing them without verification is misconduct by an advocate, and directed the BCI to frame guiding principles.

Responsible AI & professional guardrails

AI changes the drafting. Not who answers for it.

A neutral summary of the rules the course works within. It is not legal advice; the Advocates Act, the BCI Rules and your firm's policies take precedence. Checked October 2026.

Five rules we never bend
  1. No privileged or client-identifiable material in public AI tools.Anonymise, or use a tool the firm has approved under contract.
  2. Read every case you cite.Check the judgment exists, says what is claimed and is still good law, in an official or authoritative source.
  3. AI output is a draft.The advocate edits, approves and signs, and is responsible for every word filed or sent.
  4. Map old sections to new.IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act references must be checked against BNS, BNSS and BSA where the new laws apply.
  5. Human-verify every translation.A regional-language document used in a matter is checked by a competent person, not trusted as machine output.
BCI Rules · Part VI, Ch. II, Rule 17Duty to the client

An advocate must not, directly or indirectly, breach the obligations of section 126 of the Evidence Act, the privilege now carried in section 132 of the BSA.

BSA, 2023 · ss. 132–134Professional communications

Section 132 covers professional communications, section 133 says privilege is not waived by volunteering evidence, and section 134 covers confidential communications with legal advisers.

BCI Rules · Rule 36No solicitation or advertising

Advocates may not solicit work or advertise, directly or indirectly. The course does not teach AI for client acquisition, and this page is a training offer, not an advocate's advertisement.

Supreme Court · 2026 INSC 668Verify before you cite

Zero tolerance for producing, citing or using AI-generated precedents without verification; the BCI was directed to set up a committee and prescribe disciplinary norms.

BSA, 2023 · s. 63Electronic records need a certificate

An electronic record offered in evidence is accompanied by a certificate in the Schedule form. Module 8 uses this as context for questions about AI-generated or AI-altered material.

DPDP Act 2023 · Rules 2025Client personal data

Client files hold personal data. The Rules were notified in November 2025 with an 18-month phased timeline for notice, consent, safeguards and breach intimation.
