Phrase 1 of 7

TDS NOT APPLICABLE

Refers to income-tax TDS under section 194J on the software lines of this invoice. It does not touch GST, and it does not cover service lines on the same invoice.

Not: an exemption from GST or from tax on our income.

The conditions

Three conditions, all of them required.

Notification No. 21/2012 [F.No.142/10/2012-SO(TPL)], S.O. 1323(E), dated 13 June 2012, was issued under section 197A(1F) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 and applies from 1 July 2012. It covers a payment for acquiring software from a resident seller (the transferor). No tax is deducted under section 194J on that payment only if every condition below is met.

i

A subsequent transfer, without modification

The software reaches you in a later transfer, and the seller has passed it on without any modification. A standard licence resold as is fits. Software built or altered for you does not.

Notification para 1, condition (i)
ii

Tax deducted on an earlier transfer

Tax was already deducted on a previous transfer of the same software: (a) under section 194J, or (b) under section 195 on a payment to a non-resident, for example when it was imported from the publisher.

Condition (ii), sub-clauses (a) and (b)
iii

A declaration with the seller's PAN

You, the buyer (transferee), obtain a declaration from the seller that tax was deducted under (a) or (b), together with the seller's Permanent Account Number. The invoice footer and the letter give you this.

Condition (iii)

What counts as "software"? The notification itself does not define it. For guidance, practitioners commonly refer to Explanation 3 to section 9(1)(vi) of the 1961 Act, under which "computer software" means a computer programme recorded on a disc, tape, perforated media or other information storage device, including such a programme or customised electronic data. Commentators read the notification as aimed at standard (off-the-shelf) software, not software made to order.

Software vs services on one invoice

The declaration covers software lines. Service lines still need TDS.

One XcellHost invoice can mix licences and services. Treat each line on its own. The examples below are typical only. What decides it is whether the declaration is printed against the line on your invoice, so confirm each invoice.

    Income-tax Act 2025 note

    The 1961 Act was replaced on 1 April 2026. Here is what we could verify.

    The declaration on our invoices cites a notification issued under the 1961 Act. This is where things stand on the sources we checked.

    Verified

    The Income-tax Act, 1961 stands repealed from 1 April 2026. Sums paid or credited from that date fall under the Income-tax Act, 2025, judged by the earlier of credit or payment (CBDT FAQs on the transition, Q1.2 and Q2.4).

    Verified

    Under the 2025 Act, TDS on fees for professional and technical services and royalty (the old section 194J) is in section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 6(iii).

    Verified

    The CBDT FAQs say notifications and circulars issued under the 1961 Act remain valid so long as they are not inconsistent with the new Act (section 536(2)(j), FAQ Q1.20).

    Not confirmed

    We did not find a CBDT document that names Notification 21/2012 specifically, re-issues it under the 2025 Act, or maps section 197A(1F) to a new section.

    Our reading, plainly

    The declaration references the 1961-Act notification. Check its current applicability under the Income-tax Act 2025 with your tax adviser. We will update this page when CBDT publishes something specific.
