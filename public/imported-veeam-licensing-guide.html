01The short version#

Veeam sells most of its software on one portable licence, the Veeam Universal License (VUL). You buy a number of instances, and each protected workload uses up a published share of them: one per VM or server, one per three workstations, one per 500 GB of file share data. The edition you buy (Foundation, Advanced or Premium) decides which features those instances unlock. Microsoft 365, Salesforce and Kubernetes are licensed separately.

One licence, many workload types

The same VUL covers VMware, Hyper-V, Proxmox and other VMs, physical servers, cloud VMs, workstations and NAS. You can move instances between workload types without buying a new SKU.

Only recent backups count

A workload counts as protected, and uses an instance, if Veeam created at least one restore point for it in the past 31 days. Retire a server and its instance frees up a month later.

Three paid editions, plus Essentials

Foundation is backup and recovery. Advanced adds Veeam ONE monitoring and threat analytics. Premium adds Recovery Orchestrator. Essentials is the small-business package, capped at 50 instances.

Free up to 10 workloads

Community Edition protects up to 10 workloads with no licence key and no paid support. Above that, Veeam points small businesses to Data Platform Essentials.

Sockets are legacy

New perpetual per-socket licences are no longer sold. Sockets cover only vSphere and Hyper-V VMs. Moving to VUL uses Migration SKUs that must cover every socket on the contract.

Licences on quote, services fixed

XcellHost quotes Veeam licences in rupees. Our own services have list prices: backup operations from ₹999 per workload a month and a Mumbai DR target from ₹49,999 a month, indicative and excl. 18% GST.

Tip

Before you ask for a quote, list every VM, physical server, workstation, NAS share (with its size in GB), Entra ID user count, Microsoft 365 user count and Kubernetes worker node. Tool 1 in this guide turns that list into instances in under a minute.

03The Veeam Universal License, explained#

A VUL is a pool of instances. The pool is not tied to a hypervisor, a host or a socket, so the same 50 instances can protect 50 VMs this year and 30 VMs plus 60 laptops next year. Veeam's policy calls the thing being protected a licensed object, and publishes how many objects one licence covers.

What a VUL gives you

  • Portability. Instances follow the workload across hypervisors, physical servers and clouds.
  • One key. The same licence file is installed on the backup server and consumed by every supported workload type.
  • Edition upgrades. Veeam's policy says upgrades between Essentials, Foundation, Advanced and Premium are available for all VULs.
  • Capacity Packs for big file estates. For unstructured data generally above 250 TB, Capacity Packs are sold in 1 TB steps. When a Capacity Pack is present, all unstructured data is covered by capacity licences, not by instances.
Watch out

Microsoft 365, Salesforce and Kubernetes do not draw from your VUL pool. They have their own per-user or per-node licences. A Data Platform quote for "200 instances" covers none of your mailboxes.

      Who consumes a Microsoft 365 licence (self-hosted)

      • Each protected user account uses one unit. A user's mailbox, online archive and personal site together count once.
      • Shared, resource, group and public folder mailboxes are free, but only if no Microsoft 365 licence is assigned to them.
      • Teams and non-personal SharePoint sites do not consume units themselves, but users who are members of a backed-up team or can access a backed-up site must be licensed.
      • 31 days again: an object not backed up for 31 days has its unit revoked automatically. Restores never need a licence.
      Entra ID and Salesforce

      On Veeam Data Platform, Entra ID is counted at 10 users per instance and every enabled member user must be licensed. Standalone Entra ID and Salesforce offerings start at 10 users; Salesforce counts Salesforce and Salesforce Platform user licences, and sandbox users are not licensed.         How the monthly cycle works

        1. The provider's backup servers connect to Veeam Service Provider Console, which is free.
        2. The console builds a monthly License Usage Report from the high-watermark use across all servers holding the licence.
        3. The provider submits that usage in the VCSP Pulse portal to its Veeam aggregator, which bills it.
        4. A No Commit agreement gives 75 days of use, extended 30 days for each month of reported usage. Volume Rental Agreements give longer terms in return for a monthly minimum in points (Veeam's example: 200 points).

        Renewals & co-terming

        • Subscriptions renewed within 30 days after the end extend from the original expiry date; later renewals start when the purchase order is processed.
        • Perpetual maintenance always extends from the support expiry date, so renewing early loses nothing.
        • Co-terming aligns several contracts to one expiry date. Monthly SKUs are used only for that alignment, priced per socket or per 10 VULs.

        Merging licences

        • Allowed for customers current on maintenance, in the customer portal or by support case.
        • Editions must match; the merged key takes the earliest licence and support expiry dates.
        • Essentials VULs and Capacity Packs merge only up to 50 VULs and 50 TB.

        If you bought Veeam in three separate orders, ask for a co-term quote at your next renewal. One expiry date means one renewal, one support level and one key to manage.

        18Mistakes to avoid#

        1. Counting laptops as full instances. Three workstations share one instance.
        2. Rounding NAS up instead of down. Each share rounds down to the nearest 500 GB, and shares under 500 GB use nothing.
        3. Expecting VUL to cover Microsoft 365 or Kubernetes. Both are licensed separately, per user and per node.
        4. Forgetting Entra ID users. On Data Platform, every enabled member user must be licensed, at 10 users per instance.
        5. Buying Foundation and expecting Recovery Orchestrator. It needs Premium, or Advanced with extra Orchestrator licences.
        6. Planning past 50 on Essentials. Essentials cannot be merged above 50 instances.
        7. Removing the old key first. The server drops to Community Edition until the new key is in.
        8. Buying new VULs to replace sockets without Migration SKUs. Veeam does not permit it.
        9. Letting socket support lapse 6 months. After that, the licence can no longer be renewed.
        10. Leaving automatic licence update off. It halves your overage allowance, from 20% to 10%.

        20Glossary#

        Aggregator
        The Veeam partner that holds a VCSP's rental agreement and bills its monthly usage.
        Capacity Pack
        A licence for unstructured data in 1 TB steps, generally for estates above 250 TB (Essentials: 5–50 TB).
        Co-terming
        Aligning several Veeam contracts to one expiry date, using monthly SKUs.
        Community Edition
        Free Backup & Replication for up to 10 workloads, without paid support.
        Essentials
        The small-business package of Veeam Data Platform, in packs of 5 instances up to 50.
        Gifted VUL
        One free VUL per perpetual socket, up to six, for non-VM workloads.
        High watermark
        The peak usage in a month, which VCSP rental reporting is based on.
        Instance
        The unit of a Veeam Universal License. One VM or server uses one; three workstations share one; 500 GB of unstructured data uses one.
        Kasten
        Veeam's Kubernetes backup product, licensed per worker node.
        Licensed object
        The policy's term for a protected thing: a VM, server, workstation, 500 GB of data or an Entra ID user.
        Migration SKU
        The part number used to move perpetual socket licences to VUL. It must cover every socket on the contract.
        NFR
        Not For Resale: a free licence for demos, training and education.
        Points (PPU)
        VCSP rental consumption units per workload per month, which differ by edition.
        Protected workload
        A workload with at least one restore point created in the past 31 days.
        Recovery Orchestrator
        Veeam's tool for orchestrated, tested DR plans; included in Premium.
        Rental licence
        A monthly licence available only to VCSP partners, billed on reported usage.
        Socket licence
        Legacy licence per occupied CPU socket on vSphere or Hyper-V hosts. No new perpetual sales.
        Software Appliance
        Veeam's pre-hardened, turnkey deployment of the backup server; a VUL feature.
        VCSP
        Veeam Cloud & Service Provider, the programme for providers that deliver Veeam as a service.
        VCSP Pulse
        Veeam's portal where providers generate licences and submit monthly usage.
        Veeam ONE
        Monitoring and reporting for Veeam and virtual infrastructure; included in Advanced and Premium.
        Veeam Data Cloud
        Veeam's SaaS family: Microsoft 365, Entra ID, Salesforce, Azure and Vault storage.
        VSPC
        Veeam Service Provider Console, the free console providers use to manage tenants and build usage reports.
        VUL
        Veeam Universal License: a portable pool of instances usable across workload types.

          FAQ

          Veeam licensing, answered.

          It is Veeam's portable, per-workload licence. You buy a number of instances, and any supported workload (VMs, physical servers, cloud VMs, workstations, NAS and object storage, Entra ID) consumes them at published ratios. Instances can move between workload types and platforms.

          Per the Veeam Licensing Policy v19 (June 2026): one per VM or cloud VM, one per server or application server, one per three workstations, one per 500 GB of unstructured data, and one per 10 Entra ID users.

          A workload counts against your licence only if Veeam created at least one restore point for it in the past 31 days. A machine you stop backing up frees its instance after 31 days.

          One instance per 500 GB of protected data. Each source is rounded down to the nearest 500 GB, then sources are added. A 499 GB share uses no instance; 1,490 GB uses two. Backup copy and tape jobs do not add to the count.

          Foundation is Backup & Replication for backup and recovery. Advanced adds Veeam ONE monitoring and threat analytics such as YARA scans and Threat Hunter. Premium adds Recovery Orchestrator for orchestrated, tested recovery and audit reporting, plus Coveware Recon Scanner.

          The small-business package. It is sold in packs of 5 instances to a maximum of 50, and Essentials licences cannot be merged beyond 50. Veeam points businesses that outgrow the free Community Edition to Essentials.

          Yes, for up to 10 workloads, with no expiry and no licence key. It has no paid technical support, needs telemetry, allows one installation per source infrastructure and cannot be mixed with paid licences.

          No. Veeam Backup for Microsoft 365 is licensed per protected user (minimum 10 on paid offerings), and Veeam Data Cloud for Microsoft 365 is a per-user SaaS plan with storage included. Shared and resource mailboxes without a Microsoft 365 licence do not consume a unit.

          Per Kubernetes worker node, on subscription. Kasten Free covers up to 5 nodes, and the Enterprise trial runs 60 days for up to 500 nodes. Every node must be licensed and under support.

          New perpetual per-socket licences are no longer sold. Existing socket licences keep working and can be renewed, but they cover only vSphere and Hyper-V VMs. Perpetual socket holders get one free VUL per socket, up to six, for other workloads.

          Submit Veeam's renewal request with "Renew and Migrate to VUL", stating your protected VM count (KB4550). You buy Migration SKUs that must cover every socket on the contract; in most cases support is extended by a year. Then swap the key, keeping only the VUL file.

          With automatic licence update on, a VUL allows 20 extra instances or 20%, whichever is greater, with weekly warnings after 10 or 10%. With it off, the allowance is 10 or 10%. Beyond that, extra workloads are not processed. Socket licences allow no overage.

          You get 30 days' notice and a 30-day grace period in which everything keeps working. After that, Veeam stops processing workloads and jobs fail, but you can still restore from existing backups.

          Rental licences are available only to Veeam Cloud & Service Provider partners. Usage is measured in points per workload per month (for example 11 points per VM on Foundation), reported through Veeam Service Provider Console and VCSP Pulse, and billed monthly by an aggregator.

          Production gives 24×7 coverage with a one-hour Severity 1 response target and round-the-clock work on Severity 1 issues. Basic is weekday business hours with a two-business-hour Severity 1 target. For backups you rely on, Production is the safer choice.
