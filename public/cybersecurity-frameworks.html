Core Cybersecurity & Risk: The foundations every security programme is measured against — risk management, ISMS and prioritised controls. NIST CSF 2.0 is built for organisations of every size and sector, and organises outcomes around Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover.
Security Operations & Threat: How a SOC thinks: adversary behaviour, intrusion analysis, incident handling and threat-intelligence exchange. For the XcellHost SOC, MITRE ATT&CK + NIST CSF + CIS Controls combine threat detection, control mapping and risk reporting.
Cloud Security: Controls, assurance and secure-configuration baselines for private, public and hybrid cloud. NIST's official CSF 2.0 mappings include the Cloud Controls Matrix, ISO 27001, PCI DSS and CIS Controls — so one assessment can serve several.
Zero Trust: Never trust, always verify — architecture and maturity models for identity-centric security. Zero Trust spans seven areas: Identity → Device → Network → Application → Data → Visibility → Automation.
Application Security: Secure design, verification and maturity for web, API, mobile and the software development lifecycle. Application testing combines OWASP ASVS for verification depth with the Top 10 lists for the risks attackers exploit most.
Vulnerability Management & Pen Testing: Methodologies for testing, and the scoring systems that decide what gets fixed first. Severity alone is noisy. Combining CVSS with EPSS and the KEV catalogue focuses remediation on what is actually being exploited.
Privacy & Data Protection: Privacy risk management, regulations and assurance standards that sit alongside security programmes. Several of these are laws, regulations or assurance standards rather than frameworks — but every enterprise compliance programme has to cover them.
Financial Services: Especially relevant for banks, NBFCs, payment companies, brokers and fintechs. BFSI and NBFC customers typically need RBI or SEBI alignment first, with ISO 27001 and PCI DSS as the evidence backbone.
India-Specific Requirements: The directions, guidelines and laws that apply to organisations operating in India — our home ground. CERT-In publishes dedicated guidance including its 15 Elemental Cyber Defence Controls for MSMEs (released September 2025) and cybersecurity audit-policy guidelines.
Critical Infrastructure: Resilience and security frameworks for energy, utilities, transport and essential services. Critical-infrastructure programmes pair a governance framework with business-continuity (ISO 22301) and sector rules.
OT / ICS Cybersecurity: Protecting plants, factories and industrial control systems, where availability comes first. OT security starts with asset visibility and segmentation, mapped to IEC 62443 zones and conduits.
AI Security & Governance: Increasingly important for XcellHost Cloud + AI — governing and securing AI and LLM systems. NIST is developing guidance on using AI with CSF 2.0; as of September 2026 a CSF Quick-Start Guide on AI-assisted CSF analysis and reporting is under public comment.

Cybersecurity framework FAQs

What is a cybersecurity framework?
A cybersecurity framework is a structured set of practices, controls and outcomes for managing cyber risk — such as NIST CSF 2.0, ISO/IEC 27001 or CIS Controls. XcellHost maps its SOC, VAPT, cloud security and compliance consulting to 113 frameworks, standards and regulations across 12 domains, including India's CERT-In directions, RBI and SEBI requirements and the DPDP Act, so one programme satisfies every auditor and customer.
Which cybersecurity framework should my organisation start with?
Most organisations start with NIST CSF 2.0 for structure and ISO/IEC 27001 for certification, then add sector rules — RBI or SEBI for financial services, the DPDP Act for personal data, CERT-In directions for everyone operating in India.
What is the difference between a framework, a standard and a regulation?
A framework (NIST CSF, CIS Controls) organises good practice; a standard (ISO 27001, PCI DSS) sets auditable requirements; a regulation (DPDP Act, CERT-In directions, GDPR) is legally binding. Most programmes combine all three.
Can one assessment cover several frameworks?
Yes. Controls overlap heavily — NIST publishes official CSF 2.0 mappings to ISO 27001, CIS Controls, PCI DSS and the CSA Cloud Controls Matrix — so we assess once and map the evidence to each framework you need.
How does XcellHost use MITRE ATT&CK?
Our SOC maps detections and incidents to ATT&CK techniques, so you can see which attacker behaviours are covered and where the gaps are, and we report that coverage against NIST CSF functions.
What do CERT-In directions require?
Among other things, reporting specified cyber incidents to CERT-In within six hours of noticing them, synchronising clocks and keeping logs for 180 days within India. XcellHost's SOC and log retention support these obligations.
How do you prioritise vulnerabilities?
We combine CVSS severity with EPSS exploit probability and CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue, so the fix list starts with what attackers are actually using.
Do you help with AI governance?
Yes — AI risk assessments against the NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001, and security testing of LLM applications against the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications and MITRE ATLAS.
Is XcellHost itself certified?
Yes. XcellHost is certified to ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 20000-1, so the controls we recommend are the ones we operate.
