    Overview · from Industry 4.0 to 5.0

    Industry 4.0 connected the machines. Industry 5.0 asks what that does for people, the planet and the plant.

    Industry 4.0 brought sensors, connectivity, data and automation to the shop floor. In January 2021 the European Commission set out Industry 5.0, which keeps that technology but changes the goal: industry should not chase efficiency alone. It should put workers' wellbeing and skills at the centre, use resources within planetary limits, and be able to absorb shocks such as supply disruptions. The Commission describes it as building on Industry 4.0, not replacing it.

    For an Indian plant this is practical. XcellHost retrofits sensors on the machines you already run, processes data at the edge, links the shop floor to MES and ERP (including SAP), and then adds the use cases that pay back: a live digital twin, predictive maintenance, vision inspection, cobots that do the lifting while operators do the judging, and energy and carbon per unit. OT security is designed in from the start, and the platform is run by our 24×7 NOC and SOC from Mumbai.

    CCTV, biometrics and operator performance data are personal data. We design notice, purpose limits, access control and retention into shop-floor systems.

    DoT's June 2022 guidelines let enterprises run captive non-public networks: lease spectrum from a telco, take it directly from DoT, or have a telco build or slice one.

          Why predictive maintenance pays

          Catch it at P, not at F.

          Most failures give warning. Vibration and current signatures change weeks before a bearing gets hot or noisy. Sensors and models spot that early change, so maintenance is planned into a shift change instead of stopping the line. The curve is the classic reliability concept; how much warning you get depends on the failure mode.
