    Overview

    Your network, run like a utility — by people who watch it all night.

    Managed network operations means handing the day-to-day running of your network — the links between offices, the firewalls at each edge, the switches and Wi-Fi inside, and the rules that decide who and what may connect — to a team whose job is to keep it healthy. Faults are spotted by monitoring rather than by users, fixed against a written runbook, and every change goes through review before it touches production.

    XcellHost runs that service from its 24×7 NOC and SOC in Mumbai, following ITIL practices certified under ISO 20000-1. We work with the equipment you already own or supply it through our catalogue, take ownership of ISP tickets, keep configuration backups and logs for compliance, and bill per site in rupees with a GST invoice. Your IT team keeps the decisions; we take the alerts, the 2 a.m. calls and the paperwork.

        Operating model

        ITIL 4 practices, written down as runbooks — so the fix doesn't depend on who's on shift.

        Five practices carry the work. Automation handles the repeatable first steps ("shift-left"), engineers handle judgement.

        Monitoring & event management

        SNMP, flow and syslog from every device; thresholds tuned per site so alerts mean something. Duplicate events are correlated into one.

        Incident management

        Priority set by impact and urgency, engineer assigned, customer told. Runbook steps first, escalation to L2/L3 on a clock.

        Problem management

        Repeat faults get a root-cause review and a permanent fix — a flapping link replaced, a bad firmware rolled back.

        Change enablement

        Standard, normal and emergency changes. Firewall and routing changes carry a risk check, your approval and a rollback plan.

        Service request management

        New site, new VLAN, guest Wi-Fi, a port opened — logged as requests with agreed fulfilment times.

        Shift-left automation

        Scripts run the first checks (ping, interface counters, config diff) and attach results before an engineer opens the ticket.

        Cyber incidents must be reported within 6 hours and ICT logs kept for 180 days within India. We retain firewall, VPN, NAC and device logs in India and our SOC helps you meet the 6-hour reporting window.

        DPDP Act 2023

        Segmentation, access control and logging are the network side of "reasonable security safeguards". Breach timelines are supported by our incident records.
