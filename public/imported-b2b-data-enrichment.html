The basics

What data enrichment is, and why every list goes stale.

Data enrichment takes a record you already have, usually a name and a company, and adds the fields you are missing: a work email, a phone number, a job title, the company's size and industry. It also replaces values that have gone out of date.

Three jobs enrichment does

Fill gaps

Add the fields a form, event list or CRM import never captured, so reps can actually reach the person.

Correct

Replace an email that now bounces or a title that changed, and mark people who have moved company.

Qualify

Add company context such as size band, industry and location, so leads can be scored, routed and prioritised.

Why contact data decays

People change jobs, and a work email usually stops working when they leave. Titles change with promotions. Companies merge, rebrand and move domains. Direct lines are reassigned.

Waterfall enrichment

Ask one provider, then the next. Stop at the first good match.

No single data provider holds everyone. A waterfall sends each contact to several providers in a fixed order and stops as soon as one returns a usable result. Pick a scenario to watch one contact go through it.

    1Normalise the input

    Clean the name, resolve the company to its domain, and skip the contact if it is on your opt-out list.

    2Ask provider 1

    The provider most likely to hold this kind of contact goes first. The order can differ by country or field.*

    3On a miss, ask the next

    A miss moves the contact down the list. A miss costs you nothing.*

    4Stop at the first match

    Later providers are not queried, which keeps cost and response time down.

    5Verify the result

    The email gets syntax, domain, mailbox and catch-all checks. The phone gets a line-type check and, where possible, an ownership check.

    6Screen and deliver

    The opt-out list is applied again, then the record goes out with its source and the date it was checked.

    Verification

    What each result status means, and whether to send.

    A found email is not the same as a deliverable one. Each email comes back with a status, and each phone with a line type. Use them to decide what goes into a sequence or a dialler.

    1Syntax2Domain & MX records3Mailbox handshakeno email is sent4Catch-all probe5Role & disposable flags
    Valid

    The mailbox exists and accepts mail

    The domain has mail servers and the server confirmed this address during the handshake.

    SendingYes. Safe for sequences, within normal volume.
    Catch-all

    The domain accepts every address

    The server says yes to any address at the domain, so this mailbox cannot be confirmed. Many corporate domains are set up this way.

    SendingWith care. Send in small batches, watch bounces and keep these out of high-volume first touches.
    Risky / unknown

    No clear answer

    The server timed out or deferred the check, or the address is a role inbox (sales@, info@) or a disposable one.

    SendingHold. Re-check later or reach the person another way.
    Invalid

    It will hard-bounce

    The mailbox or domain does not exist, or the address is malformed. Hard bounces damage your sender reputation.

    SendingNever. Suppress it and, if needed, look for the person's new company.

    Status names vary between tools. Whether catch-all and risky results use credits: confirm with XcellHost*.

    Calling Indian numbers: DND and number series

    Indian subscribers record their call and SMS preferences in the National Customer Preference Register, through 1909 or the TRAI DND app, and can block promotional calls by sector. A B2B purpose does not change this, because the rules follow the number.

    • 140 series for promotional calls. Use numbers allotted to registered telemarketers. Customers can block 140-series calls by sector through DND.
    • 1600 series for service and transaction calls. BFSI entities regulated by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA, and government bodies, must use 1600xx numbers for service and transaction calls to existing customers. These calls cannot be tagged, blocked or filtered.
    • Registered headers for SMS. Senders register on the operators' DLT platforms. Each header carries a suffix showing whether the message is promotional, service, transactional or government.
    • 90 days after an opt-out before you can ask that person for consent again. Recipients can complain up to 7 days after receiving a message.
    Before any promotional call

    Check the number's DND preferences through your registered telemarketer, and call only from a 140-series line. Whether XcellEnrich returns a DND flag: confirm with XcellHost*.

    Sources: TRAI press release 11/2025 (12 Feb 2025) on the TCCCPR 2018 amendments; TRAI press release 91/2026 (10 Jul 2026) on the 1600 and 140 series. Not legal advice.

    Data hygiene playbook

    Enriched data stays useful only if you look after it.

    Four habits keep a contact database clean, plus the sending rules the big mailbox providers now enforce. Do these and your bounce and spam numbers stay where they should be.

    01

    Deduplicate first

    Merge duplicates before you enrich, so you do not pay for the same person twice.

    • Match on work email first (lower-case, trimmed)
    • Then on LinkedIn URL
    • Then on name plus company domain (fuzzy match, reviewed)
    • Keep the newest verified value and its history
    02

    Normalise formats

    One format per field makes matching, routing and reporting work.

    • Phones in E.164: +919812345210
    • Domains bare: acmepumps.in, not https://www.…
    • Countries as ISO codes (IN, AE, GB)
    • Titles mapped to seniority and department picklists
    • Company suffixes made consistent (Pvt. Ltd. / Private Limited)
    03

    Re-check on a cadence

    A suggested schedule. Tune it to your own bounce data.

    Before every new sequenceRe-verify the emails in it
    Every quarterEmail and title for records untouched for 6 months
    At 12 monthsRe-check or delete records that never engaged
    After a hard bounceSuppress, look once for the new employer, then delete
    04

    Watch the two rates

    Mailbox providers publish a spam-complaint limit, not a bounce limit. So set your own stop rule for bounces.

    < 0.3%Spam rate limit in Gmail Postmaster Tools; Google advises staying under 0.1%
    2%An example internal stop rule: pause a send when hard bounces pass it (our suggestion, not a provider rule)

    Lawful use · checked 17 Nov 2026

    Enriched contacts are still personal data. Use them lawfully.

    A work email or mobile number identifies a person. These are the rules that usually apply to B2B prospecting from India, and what each means in practice. A summary, not legal advice: confirm your position with counsel.

    India

    Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 & Rules 2025

    The Act covers digital personal data, including work contact details. Section 3(c) lists what it does not cover. Clause (ii) is the one enrichment users ask about:

    "(ii) personal data that is made or caused to be made publicly available by—
    (A) the Data Principal to whom such personal data relates; or
    (B) any other person who is under an obligation under any law for the time being in force in India to make such personal data publicly available."

    Illustration in the Act: "X, an individual, while blogging her views, has publicly made available her personal data on social media." The Act does not apply in that case.

    What it means for enrichment. The exclusion turns on who made the data public. Contact details that providers have compiled, inferred or bought were usually not published by the person, so plan as if the Act applies. Keep a source on every record so you can show where the data came from.

    If the Act applies

    • NoticeSection 5: say what data you hold, why, and how to withdraw consent or complain. The detailed notice rules (Rule 3) apply from 13 May 2027.
    • ConsentSection 6(1): free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action. The "legitimate uses" in section 7 do not include marketing.
    • Purpose limitationConsent covers only the data needed for the stated purpose. Data enriched for one campaign is not automatically fine for the next.
    • WithdrawalSection 6(4): withdrawing must be as easy as giving consent. Then stop, and have your processors stop.
    • Erasure & rightsSection 8(7): erase data once the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn, unless a law requires you to keep it. Sections 11–14 give rights to access, correction, erasure and grievance redress.
    • Consent ManagersSections 6(7)–(9): people can give, manage and withdraw consent through a Consent Manager registered with the Data Protection Board. Rule 4 has applied since 13 Nov 2026.
    • PenaltiesUp to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (Schedule to the Act).
    11 Aug 2023Act receives assent
    13 Nov 2025Rules notified (G.S.R. 846(E)); Board provisions in force
    13 Nov 2026Consent Manager rules (Rule 4) in force
    13 May 2027Notice, consent, rights, security and other fiduciary duties apply
    EU / UK

    GDPR: legitimate interest & Article 14

    B2B outreach can rely on legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)) if it passes a three-part test: a real purpose, processing that is necessary for it, and a balance that does not override the person's interests. Recital 47 accepts that direct marketing may be a legitimate interest.

    When data does not come from the person, Article 14 requires you to tell them who you are, your purpose and legal basis, their rights and the source of the data. You must do this within a month, or at the latest in your first message if you use the data to contact them. An objection to direct marketing (Article 21) must always be honoured. National e-marketing laws may still require consent for email.

    India

    TRAI TCCCPR 2018, amended 12 Feb 2025

    The rules follow the phone number, so they apply to B2B calls and SMS too.

    • Promotional calls only from 140-series numbers; service and transaction calls from 1600-series numbers
    • Senders and telemarketers register on the operators' DLT platforms, with physical and biometric verification
    • Complaints can be made up to 7 days after a message; 5 complaints in 10 days trigger action
    • No new consent request for 90 days after an opt-out
    • First violation: outgoing services barred for 15 days. Repeat: disconnection for a year and blacklisting
    USA

    CAN-SPAM Act

    Applies to commercial email, B2B included. There is no consent requirement, but every message must:

    • Use accurate From, To and routing headers
    • Have a subject line that is not deceptive
    • Identify itself as an advertisement
    • Include a valid postal address
    • Offer a working opt-out, honoured within 10 business days

    You remain responsible if an agency or tool sends for you.

    Retention

    Keep enriched contacts only while the purpose lasts. Give every list a review date, delete records that never engaged, and erase on withdrawal or request (DPDP section 8(7)). Keep the suppression record: it is what stops the person being added again.

    Glossary

    The terms, in plain words.

    20 terms
    Data enrichment
    Adding missing or updated fields, such as email, phone, title or company size, to a record you already hold.
    Waterfall enrichment
    Querying several data providers in a fixed order and stopping at the first usable result.
    Credit
    The unit of usage: 1 for a work email revealed, 5 for a phone number revealed.
    Hit rate (find rate)
    The share of contacts for which a field is found. It varies with country, sector and seniority.
    Catch-all domain
    A mail domain that accepts mail for any address, so a single mailbox cannot be confirmed. Also called accept-all.
    Hard bounce
    A permanent delivery failure, for example because the mailbox does not exist. Suppress the address.
    Soft bounce
    A temporary failure, such as a full mailbox or a server that is down. Retry, and suppress if it keeps happening.
    Role address
    A shared inbox such as info@ or sales@ rather than a person's own. Lower reply rates and a higher complaint risk.
    Direct dial
    A phone line that reaches a person's own desk without going through reception.
    E.164
    The international phone number format: + then country code then number, for example +919812345210.
    Firmographics
    Company attributes such as industry, size band, revenue band and HQ location.
    Technographics
    The software and technology a company is known to use.
    ICP
    Ideal customer profile: the kind of company most likely to buy from you and stay.
    Suppression list
    People who must not be contacted or re-enriched, usually because they opted out. Kept across every channel.
    SPF · DKIM · DMARC
    Email authentication. SPF lists the servers allowed to send, DKIM signs each message, and DMARC tells receivers what to do when checks fail.
    DLT
    The operators' registration platforms in India for commercial SMS and call senders, their headers and message templates.
    DND / NCPR
    India's Do Not Disturb preferences, held in the National Customer Preference Register and set through 1909 or the TRAI DND app.
    Data Fiduciary
    Under the DPDP Act, whoever decides why and how personal data is processed. For outreach, that is usually you.
    Consent Manager
    A Board-registered entity through which people give, manage, review and withdraw consent under the DPDP Act.
    Legitimate interest
    A GDPR legal basis (Article 6(1)(f)) that needs a documented balancing test. The DPDP Act has no general equivalent for marketing.
