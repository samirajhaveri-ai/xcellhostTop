Overview

A continuity plan that works on the worst day, not just in the audit file.

ISO 22301:2019 is the international standard for a business continuity management system (BCMS). It asks you to work out which activities matter most, how long each can stop before the damage is unacceptable, and what you will do to restart them — then to write it down, rehearse it and keep improving it. The core is the business impact analysis: for each activity you set a maximum tolerable period of disruption (MTPD), a recovery time objective (RTO), a recovery point objective (RPO) and the minimum level you must run at (MBCO).

In India the triggers are familiar: monsoon flooding, grid and DG failures, ransomware, telecom cuts and a single vendor everyone depends on. Regulators expect a tested plan — RBI for banks and NBFCs, SEBI for market intermediaries, IRDAI for insurers. XcellHost runs the programme end to end from Mumbai, links it to your ISO 27001 controls and IT DR, and prepares you for an audit by an accredited certification body. We prepare you; the certificate is issued by the certification body.

    Questions, answered.

    ISO 22301:2019 is the international standard for business continuity management systems. It sets out how to understand what your organisation must keep running, plan for disruption, rehearse the plans and improve them over time. It is certifiable: an accredited certification body audits you and issues a certificate valid for three years.

    No. XcellHost is the consultant: we build and prepare your BCMS. The certificate is issued by an independent certification body accredited under the IAF system (for example by NABCB in India). We help you shortlist bodies, prepare for the audits and close any findings. Their fees are paid to them directly.

    Disaster recovery restores IT systems and data. Business continuity keeps the business serving customers — people, premises, suppliers, workarounds and communications as well as IT. DR is one part of a continuity plan, and its targets should come from the business impact analysis.

    MTPD is the longest an activity can be disrupted before the impact is unacceptable. RTO is the target time to resume it, always inside the MTPD. RPO is how much data you can afford to lose, measured back from the disruption. MBCO is the minimum level you must operate at while you recover.

    For a single-site organisation of up to a few hundred people, about 14–16 weeks to be audit-ready, then Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits on the certification body's schedule. A BIA and BCP without certification typically takes about 8 weeks. Multiple sites and slow internal sign-offs add time.

    No — ISO 22301 stands on its own. If you already have ISO 27001, the two share the same clause structure, so policy, risk method, internal audit and management review can be combined. ISO 27001:2022 controls A.5.29 and A.5.30 (information security during disruption, ICT readiness for business continuity) are covered by the same work.

    At planned intervals and after significant changes — the standard leaves the frequency to you. In practice most organisations run at least one tabletop and one technical DR test a year, and regulated entities often more. Our exercise programme covers four exercises a year.

    A BCMS built to ISO 22301 gives you most of the evidence these regulators look for: a board-approved policy, a BIA with recovery objectives, tested plans, DR drills and review records. Each regulator also has specific rules, such as drill frequency or reporting formats, which we map during scoping. It is not legal advice.

    The certificate runs for three years, with surveillance audits in years one and two and a recertification audit in year three. You keep exercising, auditing and reviewing in between. Our annual exercise programme and internal audit add-on are designed for exactly that cycle.
