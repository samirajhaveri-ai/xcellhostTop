Executive summary

Enter Canada through MSP partners, with data hosted in Canada

The case on one screen: what the market is worth, why the timing works, how to enter and what we aim to reach in 18 months.

The opportunity
US$26.9B

Cloud, cyber security and IT training in Canada in 2025, summed from three analyst estimates135. That is about or . It uses the lower cloud estimate; treat it as an order of magnitude.

  • Cloud: US$16.7B–54.8B in 2025. The two estimates use very different scopes12.
  • Cyber security: US$4.0B–8.5B in 2025, by two sources43.
  • IT training: US$1.66B in 2025, growing 2.9% a year to 20345.
  • AI headroom: 12.2% of Canadian businesses used AI to make goods or deliver services in the 2025 survey, double the 6.1% of 20249.
Why now
7 dates

Seven dated compliance and technology milestones fall between January 2027 and the end of 2035, on top of three new laws and bills from 2026. Each one starts a project. See the trigger calendar.

  • Bill C-8 received royal assent on 15 Jun 2026. Its Critical Cyber Systems Protection Act will apply to designated operators in finance, telecoms, energy and transport in phases1819.
  • OSFI: full adherence to Guideline E-21 on operational resilience was due on 1 Sep 2026. AI model-risk Guideline E-23 applies from 1 May 20272527.
  • Bill C-36, tabled on 15 Jun 2026, would replace PIPEDA's privacy rules, with penalties up to CA$10M or 3% of global revenue21.
  • Threat level: 1 in 6 businesses were hit by a cyber incident in 2023, and recovery cost them CA$1.2B12.
Recommended entry model
Partner-led

Canadian MSP and MSSP resellers plus a Toronto partner DC first. Open a Canadian entity between month 6 and 12.

  • Host customer data in XcellHost's Toronto partner DC (availability is confirmed at order). Source a second Canadian site, ideally Montreal, for DR.

  • Run tooling, playbooks and the second line from the Mumbai 24×7 NOC & SOC, with logged access and data kept in Canada.

    Bill C-8 received royal assent on 15 June 2026. Its Critical Cyber Systems Protection Act applies to operators the government designates in finance, telecoms, energy and transport, and comes into force in phases. Those operators must run a cyber security programme, manage supply-chain and third-party risk and report incidents within 72 hours, with penalties of up to CA$15 million per violation per day. Suppliers should expect these duties in their contracts once the regulations are made.

    Bill C-27 died in January 2025. Its successor, Bill C-36, the Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act, was tabled on 15 June 2026 and is at first reading. It would replace PIPEDA's privacy rules and create a Digital Safety and Data Protection Commission, with penalties of up to CA$10 million or 3% of global revenue. Unlike C-27, it carries no general AI act. Until it passes, PIPEDA and provincial laws such as Quebec's Law 25 apply.

    B-13 on technology and cyber risk has applied since 1 January 2024, and B-10 on third-party risk since May 2024. E-23 on model risk, including AI models, applies from 1 May 2027. A provider to a bank or insurer should expect questions under all four.

    There is no general AI law. The Artificial Intelligence and Data Act lapsed with Bill C-27 in January 2025. The national AI strategy, AI for All, released on 4 June 2026, plans targeted laws on deepfakes, surveillance pricing, chatbot safety and election interference, plus a Canadian Trusted AI Certification. Sector rules still apply: OSFI's E-23 covers AI models at banks and insurers from May 2027.
