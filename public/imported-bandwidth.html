FAQ

Bandwidth, answered.

Metered bandwidth bills the volume you send, in GB or TB a month. Unmetered bandwidth bills a port or committed rate, and you can use it fully all month without the bill changing. Metered suits low-average, bursty traffic; unmetered suits steady high use.

Your port's rate is sampled every 5 minutes, about 8,640 samples in a 30-day month. At month end the samples are sorted, the highest 5% (432 samples, roughly 36 hours) are ignored, and the next value is your billable rate. Short peaks are free; long ones are not.

The commit is the rate you always pay for, used or not. Burst is traffic above the commit, up to the port speed. On a burstable line, if your 95th percentile is above the commit, the difference is billed at the same per-Mbps rate*. If it is below, you pay the commit.

Held for a full 30-day month, 100 Mbps moves about 32.4 TB (decimal). One TB a month is about 3.09 Mbps on average. Real traffic is rarely flat, so a port is sized for the peak while a metered plan is sized for the average. The converter on this page works it out both ways.

Each server's included transfer goes into one shared pool for your account, so a busy server can use what a quiet one does not. You pay only when the whole pool runs out. XcellCloud VM outbound traffic is fair-use pooled with your compute, and extra pooled transfer is an indicative ₹999 per TB*.

On metered and pooled plans, inbound transfer is free. On port-based plans, the 95th percentile is normally measured on both directions and the higher one is used. Your proposal states which direction is billed.

IP transit is the dedicated, BGP-capable form of bandwidth, with your own ASN and prefixes. This page covers every way to buy bandwidth, including pooled transfer for cloud servers and shared internet. Per-Mbps rates are the same as on the IP Transit page.

Flow telemetry from the edge is watched for volumetric floods. An attacked address can be blackholed with a BGP community, and larger attacks diverted to scrubbing* so clean traffic still reaches you. It is ₹9,999 per protected circuit a month (indicative). Layer-7 attacks need a WAF.

Peering at an internet exchange keeps traffic to Indian networks on a short, local path. NIXI, the National Internet Exchange of India, runs exchanges in six cities including Mumbai. XcellHost's exchange and private peering arrangements are confirmed per site in your proposal*.
