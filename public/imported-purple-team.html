      Overview

      Nothing is hidden. That is the whole point.

      A red team works in secret and asks one question: would you notice? A purple team works in the open and asks a more useful one: what did your monitoring actually see? Our testers announce each technique, simulate it safely, and sit next to your defenders while they watch the console.

      Every technique ends up in one of three buckets — detected properly, detected but buried in noise, or not seen at all. That list is the real deliverable. A miss gets a detection written on the spot, the technique is run again, and the gap only counts as closed when something fires. Two focused days of this usually move your detection coverage further than weeks of covert testing.

      Red · Blue · Purple

      Three words, three different purchases.

      They are often used interchangeably. They answer different questions, cost different amounts and leave you with different things.

      Red team

      Covert and objective-led

      A small team chases an agreed objective without warning your defenders. It tells you, realistically, whether an intrusion would be spotted.

      • Measures how good detection already is
      • Weeks of effort, most expensive of the three
      • Findings arrive at the end, in a report
      Blue team

      Your defenders

      Your monitoring, detection content and the people who respond. It is not bought as an exercise — it is the capability every exercise is testing.

      • In-house SOC, MSSP or MDR provider
      • SIEM, EDR, identity and email tooling
      • Triage and response playbooks
      Purple team

      Both, on purpose

      Attack and defence in one room, openly, with the single goal of improving detection. Cheaper than red, faster to change things, and easy to repeat.

      • Gaps fixed and re-tested during the exercise
      • Two to five days, repeatable twice a year
      • Coverage trend by ATT&CK tactic

      Rule of thumb: buy purple to improve detection; buy red to measure how good it already is. Running a red team first often just confirms the gaps a purple exercise would have found — and fixed — in two days.

      Detection engineering

      Detected, noisy, missed — and what we do with each.

      Detected properly

      The right alert, the right severity, routed to the right queue. We record the evidence so it counts towards your coverage map.

      Detected but noisy

      The rule fired, but alongside dozens of false positives. We tune thresholds, scoping and enrichment so the true alert stands out.

      Not seen

      No alert at all. First we check the telemetry exists; then we write a detection, backtest it on your history and re-run the technique.

      You keep every detection, tuning change and runbook note written during the exercise — in your SIEM, in your tenant.
