    Proactive software patch management

    Most breaches use a flaw that already had a fix.

    Microsoft, Linux distributions, Apple and application vendors publish security fixes every month — often every week. Each one has to be noticed, judged, tested, scheduled, installed, rebooted and checked on every laptop and server you own, and the ones that fail have to be undone. Miss a cycle and a known, published hole stays open. Patch management is that routine, done reliably and on time.

    XcellSecure Patch Management as a Service hands the routine to the XcellHost NOC and SOC in Mumbai. We discover what is missing, rank it by real-world exploitation, deploy in pilot-then-broad rings inside windows you approve, roll back what breaks, and send a monthly compliance, MTTP and exceptions report. It works with the tools you may already own — Intune, Acronis or Qualys — or ours. Billed in INR with GST.

    Risk-based prioritisation

    Severity alone is not urgency. Exploitation is.

    Thousands of CVEs are published each year; only a small share are ever exploited. We rank every missing patch on four public signals plus your own context, so the dangerous few go first.

    SignalWhat it tells usSourceWeight
    CISA KEV listingThe flaw is being exploited in the wild now. US federal agencies must fix new KEV entries within two weeks; we treat a KEV hit as an emergency.CISA · BOD 22-01Top
    EPSS probabilityA daily estimate of the chance a CVE is exploited in the next 30 days.FIRST.orgHigh
    CVSS base scoreHow severe the flaw is in theory: impact, attack vector, privileges needed.FIRST · NVDMedium
    ExposureInternet-facing, user-facing or isolated? Same CVE, different urgency.Your estateHigh
    Asset criticalityPayment, ERP and domain controllers outrank a lab VM.Your CMDBMedium
    Vendor risk scoreWhere you run a scanner such as Qualys, its TruRisk-style score is folded in.Scanner*Input
    How a patch is ranked
    KEV›EPSS›Exposure›CVSS

    A CVSS 9.8 that nobody exploits can wait for the normal cycle; a CVSS 7.5 on the KEV list on your VPN cannot. Priority sets the SLA clock in the SLA table.

    How it works

    Eight steps, every cycle, every device.

    1. 1Discover

      Agent or credentialed scan lists every device, OS build and installed app, and what is missing.

      inventory
    2. 2Prioritise

      KEV, EPSS, CVSS, exposure and asset criticality set a P1–P4 priority and SLA clock.

      risk-ranked
    3. 3Test ring

      Pilot devices and non-production servers get the patch first; we watch for breakage.

      pilot
    4. 4Schedule

      Servers in maintenance windows you approve; laptops with user deferral limits.

      windows
    5. 5Deploy

      Rings widen as health checks pass; snapshots or backups taken before servers patch.

      rings
    6. 6Verify & reboot

      Reboots handled, services and apps checked, patch presence re-scanned.

      proof
    7. 7Roll back

      A failed health check triggers uninstall or snapshot restore and an exception.

      safe
    8. 8Report

      Compliance %, mean time to patch, exceptions and end-of-life list, every month.

      evidence
