What Is BSIMM?

BSIMM, the Building Security In Maturity Model, is a data-driven benchmark of software security programmes. Instead of prescribing what you should do, it records the activities that participating firms actually perform, grouped into four domains and twelve practices. An assessment scores which of the 128 activities your organisation performs and plots the result against the participant pool, so you can see which practices lag and what peers in your sector do next.

Why BSIMM Matters

A Benchmark, Not An Opinion

BSIMM data comes from assessments of real companies, so you see which activities peers in financial services, technology or healthcare actually perform rather than a vendor's wish list.

Board-Level Conversation

A scorecard that compares you with more than a hundred firms turns software security budget requests into evidence-based decisions executives can follow.

Shows Regulators A Managed Programme

RBI, SEBI and CERT-In expect source code review, secure pipelines and SBOMs; BSIMM practices such as CR, SR and SE give that work a recognised structure.

Tracks Improvement Over Time

Repeat assessments show exactly which activities were added since last year, so the programme can prove progress instead of restating intentions.

Who it is for

Who Needs BSIMM?

  • Software product companies and SaaSEnterprise buyers ask how mature your software security programme is; BSIMM gives a credible answer.
  • Banks, NBFCs and fintechsFinancial services is a long-standing BSIMM vertical, so sector comparisons are meaningful.
  • IT services firms and GCCsGlobal parents and clients run BSIMM; Indian delivery centres need to show the same activities.
  • Large enterprises with in-house developmentDozens of internal applications need a single programme view rather than project-by-project testing.
  • Healthcare and IoT product makersBoth verticals appear in BSIMM data and face rising product security expectations from buyers.
What it takes

What A BSIMM-Style Assessment Involves

  1. Interview the programmeStructured interviews with security, engineering and operations leads to establish which activities are really performed.
  2. Gather evidencePolicies, tool output, training records and test reports that confirm each activity is routine, not aspirational.
  3. Score the activitiesMark each of the 128 activities as observed or not, by practice and by domain.
  4. Compare with peersPlot the scorecard against the participant pool and against your industry vertical to find the gaps that matter.
  5. Plan the next activitiesChoose the activities peers at your level adopt next, with owners, budget and dates.
  6. ReassessRepeat the assessment, typically every one to two years, to show measured progress to leadership.
🇮🇳 India context

BSIMM In The Indian Context

No Indian regulator requires BSIMM, and no Indian body certifies against it. Its value in India is as the yardstick global customers and parent companies use, and as a structure for the secure development and SBOM expectations CERT-In, RBI and SEBI now set.

CERT-In's July 2025 audit policy guidelines name source code review, application testing and SBOM review as audit areas, matching BSIMM's CR, ST and SR practices.
CERT-In's Technical Guidelines on BOMs (version 2, 2025) encourage SBOMs for government and critical-sector suppliers; SBOM adoption is a rising BSIMM16 trend.
RBI's IT governance directions and SEBI's CSCRF expect secure development practices for customer-facing applications that BSIMM's touchpoints evidence.
Indian GCCs and IT services firms are often asked to mirror the BSIMM activities their global parents report.
