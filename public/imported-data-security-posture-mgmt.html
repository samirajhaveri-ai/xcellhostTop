What DSPM is

Security that starts with the data, not the server it sits on.

Data security posture management is a continuous loop: find the data, understand it, check who can reach it, fix what is exposed and keep watching.

DSPM answers three questions for every sensitive record you hold: where is it, who and what can get to it, and where is it moving? It scans cloud databases, object storage, SaaS apps, file shares and AI pipelines, labels what it finds, and ranks exposure by how sensitive the data is and how open the path to it is.

CSPM asks a different question — is this cloud resource configured safely? A perfectly configured bucket can still hold 40 lakh Aadhaar numbers nobody knew about. DSPM finds that; CSPM would not. The two work best together.

What is shadow data?

Copies nobody is tracking: a production database cloned for testing, a CSV export left in a bucket, old snapshots and backups, a retired app's tables, a SaaS workspace a team set up on its own. It usually has weaker access controls than the original — and it still counts as personal data under the DPDP Act.

  • Dev and test clones of prod
  • Exports and reports in buckets
  • Orphaned snapshots and backups
  • Unmanaged SaaS workspaces

Classification

Detectors built for Indian data — checksums, not just patterns.

A 12-digit number is not always an Aadhaar number. Good classifiers check structure and checksums and look at the words around a match, so you chase fewer false alarms.

Aadhaar & VID12 digits, first digit 2–9, Verhoeff checksum; masked forms and 16-digit Virtual IDs
PANFive letters, four digits, one letter; the fourth letter shows holder type (P person, C company…)
GSTIN15 characters: state code, the holder's PAN, entity number, "Z" and a check character
Bank & UPIAccount numbers near an IFSC (4 letters, 0, 6 characters), UPI IDs, card numbers with a Luhn check
Health14-digit ABHA numbers, diagnoses, prescriptions and lab reports
Contact & identityIndian mobile numbers, email, passport, voter ID, plus custom types such as employee or policy IDs

    Access governance

    Most leaks are not hacks. They are access nobody took back.

    DSPM joins what the data is with who can reach it, so a broad permission on a payroll file ranks above the same permission on a lunch menu.

    Over-permissioned identities

    Users, groups and service accounts with read access to sensitive stores they have not touched in 90 days*.

    Public and "anyone" links

    Files shared with anyone who has the link, public buckets and external guests on sensitive sites.

    Stale and over-retained data

    Sensitive records kept past their purpose — a DPDP problem as well as a breach risk.

    Machine identities

    API keys, app registrations and roles that can read whole databases, flagged for your CIEM.

    Unencrypted sensitive stores

    Stores with personal data but no encryption at rest, or keys held in the wrong place.

    Owner reviews

    Each finding goes to a named data owner with one-click keep, reduce or remove — and an audit trail.

    Data lineage & residency

    See where data travels — and where Indian rules say it must stay.

    Lineage maps copies and movements between stores, apps, regions and vendors. That is the evidence you need when a regulator, auditor or customer asks where their data is.

    DPDP Act · section 16Transfers abroad are allowed — except where restricted

    The Central Government may notify countries to which personal data cannot be sent. Section 16(2) keeps stricter sectoral rules in force, so a sector regulator's localisation rule still applies.

    DPDP Rules 2025 · rules 13 & 15Conditions and significant data fiduciaries

    Rule 15 lets the Government set requirements for transfers. Rule 13 lets it require significant data fiduciaries to keep specified data in India. Most of these obligations apply from May 2027.

    RBI · storage of payment system dataPayment data stored only in India

    Payment system operators must store the full payment data in India. Processing abroad is allowed, but the data must come back to India within one business day or 24 hours, whichever is earlier.

    What we mapStore-to-store copies, ETL jobs, SaaS integrations, backups to other regions, vendor and sub-processor transfers, and AI services called from your apps.
    What we flagRegulated data in a non-India region, unknown destinations, payment data outside the allowed window, and copies that broke their source's access rules.
