Overview

Your cloud provider secures the cloud. You secure what you put in it.

AWS, Azure and Google run the data centres, hardware and hypervisors. Everything you configure on top is yours: who can sign in, which roles can do what, which buckets face the internet, whether logs are on and kept. That half is where most incidents begin, and it changes every time someone deploys.

XcellHost Cloud Security Assessment is a point-in-time, authorised review of that half. We connect with a read-only role, check every account and region against CIS Foundations benchmarks and the CSA Cloud Controls Matrix, analyse effective permissions and internet exposure, and give you a ranked list of what to fix first. When you have fixed it, we check again.

Why it matters

Indian businesses moved to cloud fast. Settings did not always keep up.

Teams spin up accounts for projects, vendors get access for a sprint and keep it, and a test environment quietly becomes production. None of it raises an alarm on its own.

01

Public by accident

Storage, snapshots and databases opened for a quick share and never closed again.

02

Too much privilege

Roles and service accounts with wildcard rights, and access keys older than the people who created them.

03

Logging gaps

Audit trails off in a new region or kept for 30 days, so you cannot meet CERT-In's 180-day expectation.

04

Account sprawl

Dozens of accounts, subscriptions and projects across teams, with no one view of what is exposed.

A breach caused by a setting is still your breach. Under the DPDP Act 2023 the data fiduciary is responsible for reasonable security safeguards, whether the data sits in your data centre or in someone else's cloud.

Methodology

Six phases, read-only throughout — nothing in your cloud is changed by us.

Automated checks find the settings. People decide which ones matter, by tracing how they combine.

    Scope, accounts and regions listed. Written authorisation, NDA and data-handling terms signed.

    Day 0
  2. 02Connect read-only

    You create a read-only audit role; we never ask for owner or write access. Access is time-boxed.

    Day 1
  3. 03Discover & inventory

    Every account, region and service enumerated, including resources nobody remembers creating.

    Day 1–2
  4. 04Configuration review

    Hundreds of checks against CIS benchmarks, then manual review to remove false positives.

    Day 2–4
  5. 05Identity & exposure analysis

    Effective permissions and internet paths, framed by MITRE ATT&CK tactic names such as Initial Access and Privilege Escalation.

    Day 4–6
  6. 06Report, readout & retest

    Ranked findings, fix guidance, an executive summary, then a retest once you have remediated.
