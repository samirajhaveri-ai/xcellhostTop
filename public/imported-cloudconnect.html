Routing & high availability

Static for two sites. BGP when it has to heal itself.

Routes decide which tunnel carries traffic; HA decides what happens when a line or a box fails.

Static routesYou list the prefixes
  • Simplest for one or two sites with one tunnel each
  • Each office range points at a tunnel; each cloud range at the gateway
  • Failover depends on dead-peer detection and route tracking on your firewall
  • Every new subnet is a change on both ends
BGP over the tunnelRouters tell each other
  • New subnets are announced, not typed in twice
  • Two tunnels, one preferred: AS-path prepend or MED picks the primary
  • With BFD, a dead path is noticed in under a second and withdrawn
  • Private ASNs (64512–65534) on your side; tunnel addresses from 169.254.0.0/16
Active / standby

Two tunnels from two public IPs. One carries traffic; the other takes over on failure. Easiest to reason about and to troubleshoot.

Default for most sites
Active / active

Both tunnels carry flows (equal-cost multipath with BGP). More usable bandwidth; each tunnel must still be able to carry the whole load alone.

Busy sites
Dual gateway, dual ISP

Each tunnel lands on a different XcellHost gateway and leaves your office on a different ISP. Survives an ISP outage and a gateway maintenance window.

    FAQ
    CloudConnect, answered.
    
    
    Connectivity from your offices, branches, plants and remote staff into your XcellHost cloud network in Mumbai. Sites join over IPsec site-to-site VPN, managed SD-WAN or a carrier MPLS handoff; people join over client VPN with MFA. Servers keep private IPs and never need to face the internet.
    

    CloudConnect is site-to-cloud: offices and people into XcellCloud. DirectConnect is dedicated private ports and cross-connects at 1 to 100 Gbps. Cloud Connectivity covers links between clouds, edge sites, Wi-Fi and IP transit. The comparison table above shows when to use each.
    

    Any firewall or router that supports standards-based IKEv2 IPsec. XcellHost engineers help configure your end at no extra charge on the self-service tunnel, and manage it end to end on the managed site link.
    

    A VPN tunnel is ₹1,999 a month and a managed site link ₹4,999 a month. Managed SD-WAN is ₹2,999 (Essential) or ₹4,499 (Professional) per site, a cross-connect for an MPLS handoff ₹2,999, and a client VPN pack of 25 users ₹2,499* (placeholder). All indicative, excl. 18% GST.
    

    Not for one or two sites with one tunnel each — static routes are simpler. Use BGP when you have HA tunnel pairs, many subnets or many sites, so routes are announced and withdrawn automatically. Your side uses a private ASN; tunnel addresses come from 169.254.0.0/16.
    

    With BGP and BFD at 300 ms × 3, a dead path is detected in about a second. With static routes, failover waits for dead-peer detection — commonly 30 to 40 seconds with 10-second probes. SD-WAN steers traffic per application as soon as loss or latency crosses the policy.
    Use the sizing tool above: it multiplies users, concurrency and per-app loads, adds nightly sync and IPsec overhead and suggests a line size. The per-user figures are planning assumptions — measure real traffic before ordering lines.
    

    Usually MTU: IPsec adds roughly 55 to 85 bytes per packet, so full 1,500-byte packets no longer fit. Clamp TCP MSS to about 1,360 and set the tunnel MTU near 1,400; the sizing tool computes exact values for your cipher.
    

    Yes. Your carrier* hands off the MPLS VPN in our Mumbai Tier IV data centre over a cross-connect (₹2,999 a month); we map it into a VRF that reaches your VPC. Keep an IPsec tunnel as a backup path.
    

    Each login needs MFA, users get only the subnets their group allows, and connection logs stay in India for 180 days. For app-by-app access without network access, ask about zero-trust options alongside the client VPN.
    

    The hub and your VPC are in our Mumbai Tier IV data centre. Traffic between your Indian offices and Mumbai stays on Indian networks unless your ISP routes otherwise; we do not copy data outside India.
    

    A tunnel can be live within days once the parameter sheet is agreed. SD-WAN sites depend on hardware delivery; MPLS handoffs depend on the carrier's cross-connect and circuit lead times.
