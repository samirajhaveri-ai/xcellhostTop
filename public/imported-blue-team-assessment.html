Overview

Buying a SIEM doesn't make a SOC. Practice does.

Most Indian organisations now have the pieces of a defence: an EDR on laptops, a firewall, Microsoft 365 alerts, perhaps a SIEM or a managed SOC. What they rarely have is evidence that the pieces work together — that the right logs arrive, that rules fire on what matters, that a tired analyst at 3 a.m. escalates the one alert that counts, and that leadership knows who calls CERT-In.

XcellHost Blue Team Assessment is a structured, authorised evaluation of your defensive capability — people, process and technology. We review configurations and evidence read-only, interview your team, observe agreed test events, and run a facilitated tabletop. The output is a maturity score per domain, a prioritised remediation plan and a retest.

Why it matters in India

Six hours is not long when nobody knows whose call it is.

CERT-In expects specified incidents to be reported within six hours of noticing them. That window is spent on detection, triage, escalation and a decision — not on the report itself.

6 hCERT-In window
  • Detect · 40 m
  • Triage · 50 m
  • Escalate · 2 h
  • Decide · 1 h
  • Report · 30 m
sample split — escalation is where most time is lost
01

Alert fatigue is real

Small teams face thousands of alerts a day across EDR, email and firewall. Without tuning, the important one is buried — and analysts learn to ignore the queue.

02

Skills are thin

Many Indian SOCs run with two or three people per shift, often shared with IT operations. Knowing where to invest training is half the battle.

03

Regulators ask for proof

RBI, SEBI CSCRF and IRDAI expect periodic testing of SOC effectiveness and incident response — documented, dated and reviewed by the board.

04

Tool sprawl hides gaps

Each product reports green on its own console. Nobody checks the joins — the log that never reached the SIEM, the rule that points at a renamed field.
