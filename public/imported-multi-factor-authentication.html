    Overview

    Passwords leak. Codes get phished. The right second factor does not.

    Multi-factor authentication asks for a second proof — something you have or something you are — after the password. It is the single most effective control against stolen credentials, but not every factor is equal: an SMS code or a one-time password can be typed into a fake login page, and a push prompt can be approved by a tired user. Passkeys, FIDO2 security keys and certificate-based sign-in are bound to the real website, so a phishing page has nothing to capture.

    XcellHost turns that into a working rollout on Microsoft Entra ID: break-glass accounts first, a pilot group, a registration campaign, Conditional Access policies in report-only mode, then enforcement — and a helpdesk that handles lost phones and new joiners in Hindi or English. For VPNs, RDP and Windows logon outside Microsoft 365 we add Accops HyID or the Entra NPS extension.

    Quick answer · updated October 2026

    What is phishing-resistant MFA?

    An authentication method that cannot be relayed through a fake website, because the cryptographic key only answers the genuine site's origin. Passkeys, FIDO2 security keys, Windows Hello for Business and certificate-based authentication qualify; SMS, voice, one-time-password apps and push approvals do not. NIST SP 800-63B-4 (final, July 2025) requires phishing resistance at AAL3 and expects verifiers to offer a phishing-resistant option at AAL2.

    Why it matters in India

    Most breaches start with a login. Regulators now expect a second factor.

    Identity-led attacks rarely need malware: a reused password, a phishing kit that relays the OTP, a SIM swap or a flood of push prompts is enough. Strong MFA helps you meet what Indian regulators ask for.

    CERT-In

    6-hour reporting · 180-day logs

    CERT-In's April 2022 directions require cyber incidents — including unauthorised access — to be reported within 6 hours and ICT logs kept for 180 days in India. MFA sign-in logs help you show who signed in, from where, with what factor.

    DPDP Act 2023

    Reasonable security safeguards

    The DPDP Act and Rules expect access control and logging around personal data. Strong authentication on the systems that hold customer data is a core safeguard.

    RBI

    Two factors, one dynamic

    RBI's Authentication Mechanisms for Digital Payment Transactions Directions (25 Sept 2025, effective 1 April 2026) ask for at least two factors, with one dynamic for most digital payments, plus risk-based checks.

    SEBI · IRDAI

    MFA for critical systems

    SEBI's Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF, August 2024) makes MFA mandatory for access to critical systems; IRDAI's cyber-security guidelines set similar access-control expectations for insurers.
