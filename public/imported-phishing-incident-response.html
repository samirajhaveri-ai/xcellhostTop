Overview

Your people see the phish first. Make that count.

Filters stop most bad mail, but some always lands. When it does, the first line of defence is the person reading it. Phishing Incident Response gives that person a single button, and gives your security team everything that should happen next: the message analysed, its links and files checked, the sender's reputation looked up, and every other copy in the organisation found and removed.

It also shows you how alert your workforce really is. Who reports, how quickly, and how often they are right becomes a measurable number you can track month to month — and pair with Managed Phishing Simulation to train the people who don't report yet. XcellHost runs it on a leading platform*, connected to Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, with optional 24×7 triage from our Mumbai SOC.

Why it matters

Three facts every security team already knows.

Some phishing always gets through

No filter catches everything. Newly registered domains, clean-looking links and plain-text requests slip past even well-tuned protection.

Email is still the favourite way in

Credential theft, invoice fraud and malware delivery usually start with a message. Close that door and many attacks never begin.

Everyone with a mailbox is a target

Finance, HR and front-desk staff receive the most convincing lures. Each one who reports instead of clicking buys the team time.

How it works

From a click to a closed case — in five moves.

  1. 1 · ReportThe employee clicks Report Phish. The message moves out of their inbox and a copy goes to the queue.
  2. 2 · AnalyseHeaders, links, attachments and sender reputation checked; threat intel added; a risk score set.
  3. 3 · ClusterOther reports and unreported copies of the same campaign grouped into one case.
  4. 4 · RemoveEvery matching message quarantined across mailboxes; sender, URL and hashes blocked.
  5. 5 · CloseReporter thanked with the verdict; indicators sent to your SIEM; KPIs updated.

Marked safe in seconds, restored to the inbox, and the reporter still thanked for checking.

India compliance

Six hours starts at notice.

6 hto CERT-In

A user report is often the moment you "notice" a phishing incident. Fast analysis tells you within minutes whether the six-hour clock applies.

CERT-In · 6 hoursCERT-In's directions of 28 April 2022 require specified cyber incidents — the list includes phishing attacks and unauthorised access to accounts — to be reported within six hours of noticing them.
CERT-In · 180 daysThe same directions ask for ICT system logs to be kept for a rolling 180 days within India. Report, verdict and removal records are logged and exportable.
DPDP Act, 2023Reasonable security safeguards for personal data, and breach intimation to the Data Protection Board and affected people. A clicked phish can be where the breach starts.
RBI · SEBI · IRDAIRegulated entities face phishing, incident-response and reporting expectations under their regulator's cyber-security frameworks, including SEBI's CSCRF. A documented, measured process helps you meet them.
