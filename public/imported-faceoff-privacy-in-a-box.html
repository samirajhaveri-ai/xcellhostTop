Why FaceOff Privacy

Collect, use and protect personal data responsibly. Inside your own walls.

FaceOff describes its platform as AI-powered privacy by design: it simplifies compliance and builds digital trust as risks change. This page covers the privacy box in depth. For deepfake and synthetic-fraud defence, see the Security page.

Most DPDP tools are cloud services. That suits many companies, but not a bank whose outsourcing policy keeps customer data in-house, a department that runs on a closed network, or a hospital that will not copy patient records to a third party. FaceOff Privacy in a Box puts the whole privacy programme on an appliance in your data centre: consent records, the data map, rights requests, DPIAs, breach cases and the audit trail.

The nine modules share one unified data and consent signal, which FaceOff calls the single source of truth. When a person withdraws consent, the cookie banner, the data map, the rights desk and the evidence log all see it. You stop reconciling spreadsheets between teams.

Who does what. FaceOff designs and builds the platform and appliance. XcellHost is a FaceOff partner* and handles supply, site survey, installation, connectors to your systems, monitoring from our 24×7 NOC in Mumbai and, if you want it, DPO support through our vDPO service. You keep the data.

01

Consent Management

Capture, track and honour consent

What it does

  • Captures valid consent for each purpose.
  • Gives people self-service control over their choices.
  • Makes consent the authoritative signal everywhere.

DPDP duty

  • §5 · Rule 3Itemised notice in clear language, in English or a scheduled language
  • §6Consent that is free, specific, informed and withdrawable as easily as given
  • §6(7)–(9) · Rule 4Consent Managers: registration and duties from 13 Nov 2026
  • §9 · Rule 10Verifiable consent of a parent or lawful guardian for children

On the signal

Writes consent state and purpose scope for every person.

XcellHost maps notices and purposes with your legal team, and confirms Consent Manager interoperability with FaceOff*.

02

Cookie Management

Trackers off until people say yes

What it does

  • Discovers and classifies cookies across web and apps.
  • Blocks them until consent is given.
  • Monitors continuously and alerts on drift.

DPDP duty

  • §6Non-essential tracking needs consent for a stated purpose
  • §6(4)Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent
  • §9(3)No tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads aimed at children

On the signal

Reads consent state for each visitor, so a banner choice and a CRM record never disagree.

XcellHost scans your public sites and apps during onboarding and reviews drift alerts monthly with the vDPO service.

03

DPAR Management

Data principal access requests, on time

What it does

  • Receives data principal requests.
  • Verifies identity securely.
  • Fulfils access, correction, erasure and grievance.
  • Closes within the statutory clock.

DPDP duty

  • §11Right to a summary of data processed and who it was shared with
  • §12Right to correction, completion, updating and erasure
  • §13Grievance redressal by the Data Fiduciary
  • §14 · Rule 14Nomination; published means to exercise rights; 90-day outer limit for grievances

On the signal

Reads the live data map to find every copy, then writes the closure to the evidence trail.

Try the DPAR SLA clock below. XcellHost sets your policy SLA and escalation rules at go-live.

04

Intelligent Data Mapper

Discover and map personal data

What it does

  • Scans and maps personal data across systems automatically.
  • Gives an identity-resolved view of where data lives.
  • Detects shadow and stale data.

DPDP duty

  • §5 · §11You cannot list purposes or answer access requests without knowing where data sits
  • §8(7) · Rule 8Erase data once the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn, unless law requires retention
  • §8(1)The Data Fiduciary stays accountable, including for processors

On the signal

Writes the live data map and lineage that every other module relies on.

XcellHost plans connector waves with your IT team; see deployment & integrations.

05

Data Anonymization & Masking

Minimise and redact what you do not need

What it does

  • Anonymises or masks personal data at scale.
  • Keeps data usable for analytics, testing and support.
  • Reduces exposure without breaking operations.

DPDP duty

  • §8(5)Reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach
  • Rule 6Safeguards include encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens
  • §6(1)Process only what is necessary for the specified purpose

On the signal

Reads the data map and purpose scope to decide what to mask, and for whom.

XcellHost sets masking for test, analytics and support copies first, where exposure is usually highest.

06

PIA / DPIA Assessment

Privacy impact assessments, built in

What it does

  • Identifies privacy risk at design time.
  • Assesses likelihood and impact.
  • Recommends controls and a residual risk rating.

DPDP duty

  • §10(2)(c) · Rule 13Significant Data Fiduciaries: DPIA and audit every 12 months
  • Rule 13SDFs also check that algorithmic software does not risk data principals' rights
  • §8(5)For everyone else, a DPIA is the clearest record of reasonable safeguards

On the signal

Writes the risk posture used by Governance and Evidence.

XcellHost's DPIA service (₹49,999 per system) can run the first assessments on the box with your owners.

Data Breach Management

Detect, report and respond fast

DPDP duty

  • §8(6) · Rule 7Tell the Board and each affected person without delay
  • Rule 7Detailed report to the Board within 72 hours of becoming aware
  • CERT-InDirections of 28 Apr 2022: report specified incidents within 6 hours

On the signal

Reads the data map and lineage to scope who is affected, fast.

  • §8(1)Accountability for all processing, by you or on your behalf

On the signal

Writes the evidence trail fed by every other module.

          FaceOff Privacy in a Box, answered.

          It is FaceOff's AI-powered data privacy and compliance platform, delivered as an appliance that runs on your premises. Nine modules cover consent, cookies, data principal access requests, data mapping, anonymisation and masking, PIA/DPIA, breach, evidence and privacy governance, all on one unified data and consent signal.

          The software and the records run on hardware in your own data centre, so consent records, the data map and audit evidence never sit on a shared cloud. That suits banks, insurers, government departments and hospitals with strict outsourcing or localisation policies. If a cloud service suits you better, see our XcellPrivacy DPDP Compliance Platform.

          Notice (§5) and consent (§6) through Consent and Cookie Management; rights under §11 to §14 through DPAR Management; breach intimation under §8(6) and Rule 7 through Data Breach Management; retention and erasure under §8(7) with the Data Mapper; safeguards under §8(5) with masking; and Significant Data Fiduciary DPIAs and audits under §10 with the DPIA, Evidence and Governance modules.

          FaceOff's engine keeps one record of consent state and purpose scope, the live data map and lineage, and the risk posture and evidence trail, and returns it to every module. A withdrawal or an erasure reaches the cookie banner, the data map, the rights desk and the evidence log together.

          The DPDP Rules, 2025 set an outer limit of 90 days for grievance redressal, and you must publish how people can exercise their rights. Most organisations set a shorter internal SLA, such as 30 days. The DPAR module verifies identity, fulfils the request across mapped systems and records closure.

          Under §8(6) and Rule 7 you tell the Data Protection Board and each affected person without delay, then send the Board a detailed report within 72 hours of becoming aware. CERT-In's directions of 28 April 2022 separately require reportable cyber incidents within 6 hours. Data Breach Management tracks both.

          The Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025. Consent Manager registration and duties apply from 13 Nov 2026. Notice, consent, security, breach, retention, rights, children's data and Significant Data Fiduciary duties apply from 13 May 2027.
