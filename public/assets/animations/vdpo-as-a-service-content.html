Overview

Personal data now has a law behind it. Someone has to own it.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025 make every business that collects personal data a Data Fiduciary — responsible for notices, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting and answering people who ask what you hold about them. Large or high-risk processors may be notified as Significant Data Fiduciaries and must appoint an India-based Data Protection Officer who reports to the board.

A virtual DPO gives you that ownership without a full-time hire. XcellHost's privacy team acts as your DPO: we map where personal data lives, fix notices and consent, run impact assessments, handle requests and complaints, and lead the response when something goes wrong — then report to your management every month. Because XcellHost also runs cloud, backup and security, we can fix the technical gaps we find, not just list them.

Who needs a DPO

Every business that holds personal data needs someone accountable. Some must appoint a DPO by law.

Significant Data Fiduciaries

Must appoint an India-based DPO who reports to the board, run periodic DPIAs and audits, and appoint an independent auditor.

Every other Data Fiduciary

Must publish the contact details of a person who can answer questions about personal data — a vDPO fills this role.

Businesses serving the EU

GDPR requires a DPO for large-scale monitoring or sensitive data — and an EU-facing privacy contact.

Fast-growing digital businesses

Apps, fintech, health and e-commerce teams that collect a lot of personal data and can't wait to hire.

Why it matters in India

The DPDP Act has real penalties. Most of them are about process, not intent.

Maximum penalty · DPDP Act 2023₹250 crore

for failing to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal-data breach. Most other penalties are also about missing process — notices, breach reporting, children's data.

Failure to take reasonable security safeguardsUp to ₹250 crore
Failure to notify a personal-data breachUp to ₹200 crore
Breach of obligations for children's dataUp to ₹200 crore
Breach of Significant Data Fiduciary obligationsUp to ₹150 crore
Other breaches of the Act or RulesUp to ₹50 crore

DPDP Rules phase-in

Notified in November 2025 and phasing in over about 18 months — most Data Fiduciary duties apply from around May 2027. Mapping and consent work takes months, so start now.

Significant Data Fiduciaries

Must appoint an India-based DPO reporting to the board, an independent data auditor, and run periodic DPIAs and audits.

Children's data

Verifiable parental consent, and no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads aimed at children.

CERT-In directions

Notable cyber incidents must be reported to CERT-In within six hours — your breach plan has to cover both regimes.

RBI · SEBI · IRDAI

Sector regulators add their own data and outsourcing rules on top of the DPDP Act for regulated entities.

GDPR for EU customers

Serving EU or UK customers brings GDPR duties — one vDPO programme can cover both.
