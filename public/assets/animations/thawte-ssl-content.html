Overview

A trusted name in SSL since 1995, now on DigiCert's roots.

Thawte has issued SSL/TLS certificates since 1995 and is today part of DigiCert, so every Thawte certificate chains to DigiCert's root certificates — recognised by more than 99% of browsers, phones and operating systems. Visitors see the padlock, data in transit is encrypted with up to 256-bit strength, and nobody gets a warning page.

What sets Thawte apart is value at the organisation-validated (OV) and extended-validation (EV) levels: your registered company name goes into the certificate after a real background check, at a price below premium brands. TLS in India

Regulators assume encryption. Customers expect a name.

No Indian rule names a certificate brand, but every major framework expects data in transit to be encrypted — and OV or EV certificates add verified identity on top.

RBI-regulated entities

Banks, NBFCs and payment players work under RBI's IT and cyber-security directions, which expect strong encryption for customer data in transit. EV or OV certificates on login and payment pages make identity checks visible.

SEBI's cyber framework

Brokers, depositories and other SEBI-regulated entities must protect data in transit and manage certificates as security assets — expiry tracking is part of that.

CERT-In advisories

CERT-In regularly warns about expired certificates and outdated TLS versions. We install with modern protocols and track expiry so you're not on the wrong side of an audit.

DPDP Act, 2023

Businesses handling personal data must take reasonable security safeguards against breaches. Encrypting every form, login and API is a basic one.
