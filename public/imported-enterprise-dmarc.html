Overview · what is DMARC?

Three DNS records decide who may send as you.

DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance) tells receiving mail servers what to do with a message that claims to be from your domain but fails authentication — and sends you reports on every server using your name. It builds on SPF, which lists the servers allowed to send for you, and DKIM, which signs each message. DMARC adds alignment: the domain that passed must match the one your recipient sees.

Publishing a record is easy. Reaching p=reject without blocking your own invoices, newsletters and CRM mail is the hard part — which is why most domains stay at p=none for years. Mailbox-provider rules for bulk senders

If you send more than about 5,000 messages a day to these providers' personal accounts, authentication is no longer optional.

Feb 2024Google (Gmail) & Yahoo
  • SPF and DKIM set up for the sending domain
  • DMARC published — p=none at minimum — with the From domain aligned to SPF or DKIM
  • One-click unsubscribe on marketing mail (from June 2024)
  • Spam complaint rate kept below 0.3%
  • Valid forward and reverse DNS; TLS for transmission
Gmail stepped up enforcement from November 2025, including rejections for non-compliant traffic.
May 2025Microsoft Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live
  • SPF must pass for the sending domain
  • DKIM must pass
  • DMARC published — at least p=none — aligned with SPF or DKIM
  • Applies to domains sending over 5,000 messages a day
From 5 May 2025, non-compliant mail is rejected with error 550 5.7.515.

Summarised from Google, Yahoo and Microsoft sender guidance (checked October 2026). p=none meets the minimum; p=reject is what actually stops spoofing.
