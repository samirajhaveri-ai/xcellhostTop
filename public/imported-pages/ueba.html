What UEBA is

Not "is this rule broken?" but "is this normal for them?"

User and entity behaviour analytics watches how people and things actually behave — when a user signs in, from where, which files they open, how much data a laptop sends, which hosts a service account touches. It learns a baseline for each one, compares them with peers who do similar work, and raises a risk score when behaviour drifts.

That matters because the hardest attacks use real credentials. A stolen password, a misused admin account or an employee taking data on the way out breaks no signature. Rules written in advance miss them; a change in behaviour often doesn't. UEBA sits inside your SIEM, feeds its alerts into case management, and gives analysts a timeline instead of a pile of logs.

Key capabilities

Eight things UEBA does that a rule can't.

Per-entity baselines

Working hours, locations, devices, apps, data volumes and hosts touched — learned separately for each user and machine.

Dynamic peer groups

Users grouped by role, team and shared resources, so "unusual" is judged against people who do the same job.

Risk scoring with decay

Small anomalies add up; old ones fade. Analysts start at the top of a ranked list, not an alert queue.

Entity timelines

Everything one user, device or account did, in order, with the anomalous moments marked.

Non-human entities

Service accounts, servers, workloads and AI agents get baselines too — they are often the quietest way in.

First-seen & rarity

First access to a share, first use of an admin tool, a country no one in the team has signed in from.

Pseudonymised analytics

Analysts see U-3F9A, not a name. Unmasking needs two approvers and is logged.

Response hooks

High scores open a case and can trigger playbooks — step-up MFA, session revoke, device isolation — after approval.

Privacy by design

Watch for risk, not for people.

UEBA is a security control, not a productivity tracker. We set it up so it can find a compromised account or a data theft without turning into workplace surveillance.

  • Pseudonymised by default. Names, emails and device owners are replaced by stable tokens in every dashboard and alert.
  • Two-person reveal. An analyst and an approver you name (HR, legal or the CISO) must agree before an identity is shown — and the reveal is logged.
  • Security signals only. Sign-ins, access, data movement and admin actions. No keystrokes, screenshots, webcam or message content.
  • Retention you set. Behaviour data kept for the period your policy and CERT-In's 180-day log rule need, then deleted.
  • Notice & DPIA. Employee notice and a data-protection impact assessment template, aligned to the DPDP Act, 2023.

Where UEBA ends and workforce analytics begins

UEBA looks for security risk in individual behaviour and is worked by the SOC. Workforce Analytics looks at how teams work, only in aggregate. We keep the two separate: different data, different access, different purpose.

For the full insider-risk programme — HR process, DLP, privileged access and investigations — see Insider Threat.
