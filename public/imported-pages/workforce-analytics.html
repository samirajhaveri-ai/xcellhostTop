Our approach

Analytics about work, not surveillance of people.

We measure patterns in calendars, collaboration, tickets and licences, and show them to teams and leaders in aggregate. Employees are told what is collected, why, and can see their own numbers.

What we measure

  • Focus hours, meeting hours and after-hours work, by team
  • Collaboration load between teams — email, chat and meetings
  • Utilisation from timesheets, tickets and project tools
  • Office occupancy from desk bookings or badge counts
  • Licence and app adoption, to cut unused seats
  • Copilot usage and adoption by department

What we never do

  • Hidden keystroke logging or content capture
  • Covert screenshots, screen recording or webcam
  • Tracking of personal phones or home devices
  • Location tracking outside work systems
  • Reading email, chat or document content
  • Individual leaderboards or automated rankings

Privacy controls

Nine controls, on before the first report.

Each is a setting in your deployment, written into the policy and checked at our monthly review.

Aggregation threshold

No result for groups under your minimum — 5 by default, configurable upward. Microsoft's own Viva Insights and Copilot Dashboard thresholds can't go below five.

Pseudonymisation

Names and email addresses replaced with keyed IDs before analysis; the key stays with your nominated owner.

Role-based access

Leaders see departments, managers see their team in aggregate, analysts see pseudonymised data. Row-level security in every report.

Employee self-view

Every person sees their own focus, meeting and after-hours numbers first — and nobody else sees them individually.

Retention settings

Raw signals kept for a set period (e.g. 90 days), then deleted; only aggregates kept longer. Deletion logged.

Data residency

Analytics run inside your Microsoft 365 tenant, or on XcellHost infrastructure in our Mumbai Tier IV data centre.

Audit log

Who viewed which report, who changed a threshold, who exported data — kept and reviewable by your DPO.

    India legal context · checked November 2026

    Employee data is personal data. Plan for it.

    A short map of the laws that shape workforce analytics in India. Not legal advice — your counsel decides how they apply to you.

    DPDP Act, 2023 · s.7(i)

    Legitimate use for employment

    Section 7(i) lets an employer process personal data without consent "for the purposes of employment", or to safeguard itself from loss or liability. It covers what is needed for those purposes — so we tie every signal to a stated purpose.

    DPDP Act · s.8

    Safeguards, retention, erasure

    Data fiduciaries must take reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)) and erase data once the purpose is served, unless a law requires it to be kept (s.8(7)). Penalties for failed safeguards go up to ₹250 crore.

    DPDP Rules, 2025

    Dates that matter

    Rules notified 13 Nov 2025. Consent Manager provisions from 13 Nov 2026. Notice, security, retention, breach and rights duties from 13 May 2027. See our DPDP platform.

    IT Act, 2000 · s.43A

    SPDI Rules until May 2027

    Until the DPDP Act's s.44(2) takes effect on 13 May 2027, s.43A and the 2011 SPDI Rules still apply to sensitive personal data such as passwords, financial and biometric data.

    Labour Codes · 21 Nov 2025

    Working hours & overtime

    The Code on Wages, Industrial Relations Code, Code on Social Security and OSH Code took effect on 21 Nov 2025. Overtime and working-hours insights help you check practice against them.

    State law

    Shops & Establishments Acts

    Hours, rest intervals, overtime and night work for offices are also set by your state's Shops & Establishments law and its notifications. Rules differ by state; we configure your policy, not ours.

    Sources: the DPDP Act, 2023 (India Code), G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 Nov 2025 for commencement dates, and the Ministry of Labour & Employment's release of 21 Nov 2025. Transparent notice is our default even where consent is not the legal basis.
