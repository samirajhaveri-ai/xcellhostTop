What case management is

Alerts tell you something happened. Cases make sure something is done.

A security case is the single record of an incident from first alert to closed review: what was seen, who owns it, how severe it is, what was done and when, what evidence was kept, and who was told. Case management is the discipline — and the tooling — that keeps every incident moving through that lifecycle the same way, every time.

Without it, incidents live in inboxes, chat threads and spreadsheets. Deadlines are missed because nobody started the clock, evidence is lost because nobody hashed it, and the same mistake repeats because nobody wrote the review. XcellHost runs case management inside our 24×7 SOC, connected to your SIEM, UEBA and NDR and to the ticketing tool your IT team already uses.

Incident lifecycle

Built on NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 — the CSF 2.0 way.

NIST's April 2025 revision replaced the old four-phase model with the six functions of the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0. Preparation now sits in Govern, Identify and Protect; the incident itself runs through Detect, Respond and Recover — and lessons learned feed every function.

Before an incident
GovernRoles, severity matrix, regulator duties, who may approve what.
IdentifyAsset owners, crown jewels, personal-data systems, contacts.
ProtectPlaybooks, logging, backups, access — ready before they are needed.
During & after
DetectAlerts correlated, case opened, severity set, clocks started.
RespondAnalyse, contain, eradicate, communicate, report to regulators.
RecoverRestore safely, verify, close, and run the post-incident review.
Lessons learned feed back into Govern, Identify and Protect

Source: NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3, "Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management: A CSF 2.0 Community Profile", April 2025, which supersedes Rev. 2 (2012). Checked on csrc.nist.gov, 10 November 2026.

India regulator clocks

The deadlines start when you notice — so the case starts the clock.

6 hCERT-In

CERT-In directions, 28 April 2022

Specified cyber incidents must be reported to CERT-In within six hours of noticing them or being told about them. ICT system logs must be kept for a rolling 180 days within India.

72 hDPB report

DPDP Act, 2023 & Rules 2025

For a personal-data breach: tell each affected person and the Data Protection Board without delay, then send the Board a detailed report within 72 hours of becoming aware (the Board can extend this). Core duties apply from 13 May 2027.

RBIregulated

RBI-regulated entities

Banks and other regulated entities report cyber incidents to RBI under their own directions — urban co-operative banks, for example, on the DAKSH platform within six hours of detection. Your case records the timeline they ask for.

SEBICSCRF

SEBI-regulated entities

Market intermediaries follow SEBI's Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF), including incident reporting. We map its timelines into your severity matrix during readiness.

General information, not legal advice. Reporting obligations stay with your organisation; the CERT-In incident reporting retainer has XcellHost prepare reports with you. Confirm sector rules with your compliance team. Facts as on our phishing response, DPDP platform and UCB pages.
