What "NESA" means in 2026

The name stuck. The rulebook moved on.

Buyers still say "NESA compliance". The authority behind the name now goes by another name, and the controls sit in the UAE IA Regulation and the Cyber Security Council's IA Standard v2. Here is the lineage, with dates and sources.

  1. 2012
    National Electronic Security Authority (NESA)

    A federal authority to set e-security standards for networks and information systems and to check compliance. It is now listed as the Signals Intelligence Agency (SIA), formerly NESA 3 4.

  2. Jun 2014
    UAE Information Assurance Standards (IAS)

    NESA's first national control set: 6 management and 9 technical families, 188 controls (60 management, 128 technical), each with a priority P1–P4 5 6.

  3. Jun 2019
    National Cybersecurity Strategy 2019

    Launched by TDRA: 5 pillars and 60 initiatives, including protection of critical national assets 7.

  4. Mar 2020
    UAE IA Regulation v1.1

    Published by TDRA. Sets minimum management and technical controls; applies to all UAE entities, and critical entities designated under the CIIP Policy must implement it 1 2.

  5. Nov 2020
    UAE Cyber Security Council

    Approved by the Cabinet to build the national strategy, legal framework and a National Cyber Incident Response Plan 8. It now issues the CIIP Policy (v2.0) 9.

  6. Sep 2025
    National Cybersecurity Strategy 2025–2031

    Five pillars, including a baseline standard for all industries, a national cyber accreditation programme for security providers and data localisation for critical infrastructure 10.

  7. Oct–Nov 2025
    UAE IA Standard v2

    Released by the Cyber Security Council with new national policies. Industry analyses report 15 families retained, 134 controls and 449 sub-controls, split into "always applicable" and "based on risk" 11 12 13. We could not retrieve the official text: confirm.

Who must comply

Government, critical sectors — and the companies that serve them.

The official summary says the regulation's requirements apply to all entities in the UAE, and that critical entities designated under the CIIP Policy must implement it 1. In practice, the obligation arrives in four ways.

Government entities

Federal and local government bodies use the IA controls as their national baseline. Dubai government entities also follow DESC's ISR.

Critical infrastructure

Entities in vital sectors that are designated as critical information infrastructure under the CIIP Policy must implement the regulation.

Suppliers and vendors

IT, cloud, SOC and software suppliers inherit the controls through contracts, questionnaires and audits from government and CII customers.

Everyone else

Private companies can adopt the controls voluntarily. It is a credible baseline when you bid for UAE government or CII work.

Which sectors are designated, and when, is decided by the regulator. Check your own designation letter or sector regulator before planning to a deadline.

    The wider UAE rulebook

    IA is the baseline. Emirate, sector and free-zone rules sit on top.

    Dubai Information Security Regulation (ISR) v3

    Dubai Electronic Security Center (DESC)
    In force

    v3.0 announced Aug 2023 for all Dubai government entities, their staff, contractors and visitors. Bars storing or processing critical information outside the UAE, including in cloud; a UAE national must head information security; 13 domains 14.

    DESC CSP Security Standard

    Dubai Electronic Security Center
    In force

    Mandatory for cloud providers serving Dubai government and semi-government entities. Built on ISO 27001/27002/27017, ISR and CSA CCM; certification by DESC-accredited bodies reuses valid ISO 27001 or CSA STAR Level 2 certificates 15.

    CIIP Policy v2.0

    UAE Cyber Security Council
    In force

    Baseline security and resilience for critical information infrastructure: identifying critical assets, national risk profiles, baseline requirements, assurance and enforcement. Page updated 22 Oct 2026 9.

    ADHICS

    Abu Dhabi Department of Health
    In force

    Launched 2 Mar 2019 for all public and private healthcare facilities in Abu Dhabi; compliance due by 29 Feb 2020 and tied to licensing and Malaffi access. 11 domains; Basic, Transitional and Advanced control sets by facility type 16 17.

    CBUAE Outsourcing Regulation for Banks

    Central Bank of the UAE · Circular 14/2021
    In force

    Announced 8 Jun 2021. Material outsourcing needs CBUAE non-objection, a board-approved policy and an outsourcing register; confidential consumer data may not leave the UAE without CBUAE and customer approval 18 19.

    CBUAE Consumer Protection Regulation

    Circular 8/2020 · CBUAE Law 2025 context
    In force

    Data minimisation, documented consent, MFA for online identity checks and notifying CBUAE of material data breaches. The 2025 decree-law keeps existing regulations in force until replaced 19.

    Federal PDPL

    Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021
    Regs pending

    In force since 2 Jan 2022. Executive regulations were still unissued as of mid-2026. Excludes DIFC and ADGM entities, health data under its own law and regulated banking data 20.

    DIFC Data Protection Law 2020

    DIFC Law No. 5 of 2020 · amended 2025
    In force

    Covers processing in the DIFC. Amendments in force from Jul 2025 let data subjects go to the DIFC Courts; breach notice to the Commissioner within 72 hours 20.

    ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021

    Abu Dhabi Global Market
    In force

    Enacted 14 Feb 2021; transition for existing entities ended 14 Feb 2022 20. ADGM's FSRA also asks authorised persons to apply the national incident response plan, with an incident response rule (GEN 3.5.16) from 31 Jan 2026 21.

    Health data · Federal Law No. 2 of 2019

    ICT in health fields
    In force

    Health data may not be stored, processed or transferred outside the UAE except in permitted cases, as restated in DoH guidance 17. Abu Dhabi DoH Circular 147/2022 adds strict localisation for licensed entities 20.

    Data localisation & cloud

    Where your data can live decides who can host it.

    Three rules do most of the work. If any applies, plan for UAE hosting from day one.

    Dubai government critical data

    ISR v3 bars storing or processing critical information outside the UAE, including in cloud. Cloud providers need DESC CSP certification.

    Patient health data

    Generally stays in the UAE under Federal Law 2/2019; Abu Dhabi facilities follow DoH localisation rules and ADHICS cloud controls.

    Bank customer data

    Leaves the UAE only with CBUAE approval and the customer's prior consent under the outsourcing rules.

    FAQ

    NESA compliance, answered.

    The phrase is. NESA, set up in 2012, is now listed as the Signals Intelligence Agency, and its 2014 Information Assurance Standards became the UAE IA Regulation v1.1 published by TDRA. The Cyber Security Council has since released an IA Standard v2. When a client asks for "NESA compliance", they usually mean these IA controls.

    The official summary says its requirements apply to all entities in the UAE, and that critical entities designated under the CIIP Policy must implement it. Government entities use it as their baseline, and suppliers to government and critical infrastructure are usually bound by contract. Other companies can adopt it voluntarily.

    The control families. M1–M6 are management families: strategy and planning, risk management, awareness and training, HR security, compliance, and performance evaluation. T1–T9 are technical: asset management, physical security, operations, communications, access control, third-party security, development and maintenance, incident management and continuity. In v1.1 they hold 60 management and 128 technical controls.

    The implementation priority of each control. P1 controls are foundational and come first; P2 to P4 are progressively more advanced. Of the 188 v1.x controls, 39 are P1, 69 P2, 35 P3 and 45 P4. Beyond the always-applicable set, your risk assessment decides which controls apply.

    Industry analyses report 15 families kept, 134 controls and 449 sub-controls, a split between 70 always-applicable and 64 risk-based controls, and new controls for AI, cloud sovereignty, IoT/OT and post-quantum cryptography. We could not read the official text, so confirm these details with the Cyber Security Council's document before you plan.
