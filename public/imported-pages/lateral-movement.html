The problem

The first machine is rarely the target. It's the doorway.

Lateral movement is everything an intruder does between getting in and getting what they came for. They use your own admin tools and real accounts, so each step looks like ordinary IT work — until you line the steps up.

  1. 01Foothold

    A phished password, an infostealer log or an unpatched VPN gives access to one device or account.

    TA0001 · T1078
  2. 02Discover

    They list domain admins, servers, file shares and trust relationships — quietly, using built-in commands.

    T1087 · T1018 · T1482
  3. 03Harvest

    Cached passwords, hashes and Kerberos tickets are pulled from memory or cracked from weak service accounts.

    T1003 · T1558.003
  4. 04Hop

    Those credentials open RDP, admin shares and remote management on the next machine. Repeat.

    T1021 · T1550 · T1570
  5. 05Crown jewels

    Domain controller, hypervisors, databases and backups — then data theft or ransomware across everything at once.

    T1486 · T1490
Why it's hard to see

Every hop uses a valid account and a normal protocol — RDP, SMB, WinRM, PowerShell. Antivirus has nothing to flag. Perimeter firewalls never see it, because the traffic never leaves your network.

Why it matters

Lateral movement is what turns a single infected laptop into a company-wide outage. Stop it and a phishing click stays a one-laptop incident: reimage, reset, move on.

What we change

Fewer paths (hardening), fewer reusable secrets (identity), and eyes on what's left (NDR, UEBA, SOC) — so each hop costs the attacker time and trips an alarm.

Warning signs

What lateral movement looks like in your logs and on the wire.

Each signal alone can be innocent. Two or three on the same account or host within an hour rarely are. The tag shows where we see it.

A workstation talking to other workstations

Laptops seldom need SMB or RDP to each other. A sudden fan-out to many peers is a classic hop.

NDR · firewall logs
Admin account logging on somewhere new

A server admin interactively signing in to a laptop, or a helpdesk account touching servers at 2 a.m.

UEBA · Windows 4624
Bursts of Kerberos service-ticket requests

Many service tickets with weak RC4 encryption from one user in minutes — a sign of Kerberoasting.

DC logs · 4769
Something reading LSASS memory

Tools or scripts opening the credential store process, or creating memory dumps on a server.

EDR
Remote services and tasks created

New services, scheduled tasks or WMI/WinRM commands pushed to another machine by a user account.

Tools copied over admin shares

Executables or scripts landing in C$ or ADMIN$ on several hosts, often with the same name.

One account querying every group, computer and trust in Active Directory — far more than its job needs.

FAQ

Lateral movement, answered.

It is the stage of an attack where an intruder who has access to one device or account moves to others inside the same network — usually by reusing stolen credentials over normal admin protocols such as RDP, SMB and remote management — until they reach high-value systems like the domain controller, databases or backups. MITRE ATT&CK tracks it as tactic TA0008.

Because each step uses legitimate accounts and built-in tools, there is often no malicious file to detect, and the traffic stays inside your network where perimeter firewalls don't look. Stopping it takes identity hardening, internal segmentation and behaviour-based detection — EDR, NDR and UEBA — watched by people.

Windows can accept a password's hash instead of the password itself. If an attacker copies that hash from one machine's memory, they can sign in to other machines where the same account works — without ever knowing the password. Unique local admin passwords (Windows LAPS), credential protection on endpoints and keeping admin accounts off workstations break that chain. ATT&CK lists it as T1550.002.

A rule that admin accounts only sign in at their own level: domain admins only on domain controllers, server admins only on servers, and helpdesk accounts only on workstations. That way a compromised laptop never holds credentials that can unlock servers or the domain. It costs little and removes the most common path to domain admin.

VLANs separate broad zones; microsegmentation controls which specific servers and ports may talk to each other, even inside a zone. Start by blocking workstation-to-workstation SMB and RDP and isolating backups; then microsegment crown-jewel servers with a platform such as Akamai Guardicore* or, in the cloud, our CNAPP service.

It depends on what's deployed. With EDR on every device, identity analytics and our 24×7 SOC on the Managed bundle, alerts are triaged around the clock and hosts can be isolated through EDR without waiting for business hours. We agree response times in your contract rather than quoting a generic figure here.

CERT-In's 28 April 2022 directions list reportable incident types — including compromise of critical systems, unauthorised access to IT systems and data, and ransomware — to be reported within 6 hours of noticing. An attacker moving between your servers usually falls in that list. Our playbook prepares the evidence for that decision; the decision and filing remain yours.

If personal data is affected, you must notify the Data Protection Board and affected individuals; the DPDP Rules, 2025 set a 72-hour window for the detailed report to the Board. These breach duties apply from 13 May 2027. Showing reasonable security safeguards — least privilege, monitoring, logs — matters because penalties for failing to protect data go up to ₹250 crore.
