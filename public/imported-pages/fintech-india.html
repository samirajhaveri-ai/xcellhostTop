The market · November 2026

The world's busiest real-time payment rail, and the rulebook that came with it.

India's fintech story starts with public rails — Aadhaar for identity, UPI for payments, the account-aggregator network for consented data — and private companies building on top. UPI alone moved 25.47 billion payments worth ₹31.53 lakh crore in October 2026, about 822 million a day, according to NPCI data reported on 2 November 2026. PIB puts UPI at nearly half of the world's real-time digital payment volume, live in 11 countries.

Money is following, more selectively. Tracxn counts $2.2 billion raised by Indian fintechs in January–September 2026, up 14% on a year earlier, but across 153 rounds — 41% fewer. Digital lending and payments lead. Fewer, larger cheques go to companies that can show licences, controls and audit trails, which is why infrastructure has become a board-level topic for fintech founders.

        FAQ

        Fintech infrastructure in India, answered.

        Yes, for payment system operators and their partners. RBI's circular on storage of payment system data (6 April 2018) requires the full end-to-end transaction data to be stored only in India; data processed abroad has to come back. RBI's Payment Aggregator Directions, 2025 apply the same storage rule to aggregators.

        RBI's Payment Aggregator Directions of 15 September 2025 bring online, physical (point-of-sale) and cross-border aggregators under one framework, with ₹15 crore net worth at application and ₹25 crore by the end of the third year. They require PCI DSS compliance, an annual system and cyber audit by CERT-In empanelled auditors, half-yearly VAPT and escrow controls. Physical-only aggregators had to apply by 31 December 2025.

        RBI's Digital Lending Directions of 8 May 2025 require data to be stored on servers in India; data processed outside India must be deleted there and brought back within 24 hours. Since November 2025 these provisions sit in RBI's entity-wise Master Directions, so check the version for your bank or NBFC partner.

        RBI's Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank PSOs (30 July 2024) apply to large PSOs from 1 April 2025, medium PSOs from 1 April 2026 and small PSOs from 1 April 2028. They include six-hour incident reporting to RBI, annual VA/PT and half-yearly DR drills.

        CERT-In's directions of 28 April 2022 require reporting within 6 hours of noticing specified incidents, and ICT logs kept for a rolling 180 days within India. RBI's PSO and 2026 bank directions also set a six-hour report to RBI. Our SOC keeps the clock and drafts both reports.

        Yes. PCI DSS v4.0.1 is the current version, and the requirements that were "future-dated" became mandatory on 31 March 2025 — including payment-page script controls, authenticated internal scans and MFA for all access to the cardholder data environment. XcellHost hosts PCI-scoped workloads; your QSA assesses your compliance.

        RBI's directions on authentication mechanisms for digital payments (25 September 2025) apply from 1 April 2026. They keep two-factor authentication for most transactions but allow factors other than SMS OTP, ask for at least one dynamic factor for card-not-present payments, and allow risk-based checks. Card issuers had to apply risk-based authentication to overseas card-not-present transactions by 1 October 2026.

        RBI's framework for self-regulatory organisations in fintech (May 2024) led to FACE being recognised in August 2024 and the Unified Fintech Forum in September 2026. Membership is voluntary under the framework, but partners and investors increasingly ask about it.

        The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025. Consent-manager provisions start on 13 November 2026, and the core duties — notice, consent, security safeguards, breach intimation and rights — on 13 May 2027. See our DPDP compliance platform.
