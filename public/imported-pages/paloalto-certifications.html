What happened to PCNSA, PCNSE and the PC-series?

Palo Alto Networks says there is no direct equivalence between old and new certifications. Active legacy certificates stay valid until they expire.

How renewal and retakes work

From the Palo Alto Networks Certification Handbook, September 2026.

RuleDetail
Validity2 years from the pass date
RecertifyRetake the exam before the expiry date
Same exam againWait 18 months (545 days) after a pass
Higher tier passExtends active lower certifications in the same track by 2 years
After a failWait 15 days, then 30 days, then 90 days

So a Specialist pass can keep your Professional certification alive. Plan renewals in the tracker below.

Partner programme

NextWave levels and the certified staff behind them.

The Palo Alto Networks NextWave partner programme has four levels — Registered, Innovator, Platinum and Diamond — across partner paths for resellers, MSSPs, support and delivery services, distributors and marketplaces. Levels differ mainly in specialization and technical depth, and discounts reward certified sales and technical staff.

Specializations and focus areas (2026)

    NextWave has product, services and advanced specializations. Palo Alto says certified partners do not need to be tested every year under its continuous-education component, and a partner dashboard reaches all partners in the second half of 2026.

    Four tiers. Foundational validates basic cybersecurity concepts; Professional validates operations and management across a platform; Specialist validates deploying and running one product; Architect validates designing a secure enterprise architecture. The portfolio lists 17 certifications across Network Security, Security Operations and Cloud Security.

    No. Palo Alto Networks says there is no direct equivalence between the old and new certifications, so nothing converts automatically. You keep the legacy credential until it expires and sit the new exam you need.
