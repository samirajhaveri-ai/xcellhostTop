Who is in scope

Four regulators. One set of evidence.

A Saudi financial institution answers to the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) on cyber, IT, continuity and fraud, to the NCA where it runs critical infrastructure, to SDAIA on personal data and to the CST on cloud. We build one control set and one evidence library that serves all four.

SAMA Saudi Central Bank

Cyber Security, IT Governance, BCM and Counter-Fraud frameworks; outsourcing and cloud approvals; F.E.E.R. red teaming.

NCA National Cybersecurity Authority

ECC-2:2024 for government and critical infrastructure, CCC-2:2024 for cloud, NCNICC-1:2025 for other private firms.

SDAIA Data & AI Authority

The Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), its Implementing Regulation and the transfer regulation.

CST Communications, Space & Technology

Cloud Computing Services Provisioning Regulations for the clouds you host on.

Fintechs, payments & the sandbox

  • Entering SAMA's Regulatory Sandbox or applying for a licence: the Cyber Resilience Fundamental Requirements (CRFR, 1 Jan 2022) apply first.
  • CRFR is not a substitute: once licensed, SAMA's Cyber Security and BCM frameworks apply.
  • SAMA can refuse a sandbox graduation or licence where compliance cannot be shown.
  • Payment service providers and other licensees: requirements follow your licence conditions — we confirm them with you first.

    National rules that sit beside SAMA

    NCA controls and the PDPL apply on top.

    SAMA's frameworks do not switch off national law. Here is how the NCA and SDAIA rules land on a financial institution.

    In force

    NCA ECC-2:2024

    4 main domains, 28 sub-domains: governance, defence, resilience, third-party and cloud. Binding on government bodies and on private entities that own, operate or host critical national infrastructure. Independent review and audit required (control 1-8-2).

    In force

    NCA CCC-2:2024

    Cloud Cybersecurity Controls for providers and tenants, replacing CCC-1:2020. Regulated tenants need CCC evidence from their cloud provider.

    Applied as notified

    NCA NCNICC-1:2025

    Controls for non-CNI private firms with 6+ staff. Large firms (Category A) must use an NCA-licensed managed SOC — XcellHost holds no NCA licence, so we deliver with a licensed partner.

    Enforceable

    PDPL

    In force 14 Sep 2023; fully enforceable since 14 Sep 2024. Breaches likely to cause harm go to SDAIA within 72 hours. Transfers abroad follow the transfer regulation (amended 1 Sep 2024) and SDAIA's standard contractual clauses.

    Draft

    PDPL amendments

    SDAIA consulted on draft Implementing Regulation amendments from 6 Oct to 5 Nov 2026: storage of personal data in the Kingdom, 72-hour notice without a harm test, wider DPIA triggers and mandatory controller registration for some. Not yet final.

    Sources: NCA ECC-2:2024 (nca.gov.sa); SDAIA PDPL and Implementing Regulation; draft amendments as reported 8 Oct 2026 (Clyde & Co). NCNICC and CCC status as on our Saudi Arabia GTM briefing.

    Data location & localisation

    Can the data leave the Kingdom? Usually only with approval.

    There is no single "all financial data stays in KSA" rule. It is a set of approval gates — and the PDPL draft may add a storage rule.

    Cloud (SAMA CSF 3.4.3)

    SAMA approval before using cloud services. Cloud should "in principle" be in Saudi Arabia; outside the Kingdom needs explicit SAMA approval. Right to audit; data returned on exit; no secondary use.

    Outsourcing abroad (Rules on Outsourcing)

    Written no-objection for overseas providers, with the reason the function cannot be done in KSA, data details, a legal opinion and confirmation of SAMA's access rights.

    Personal data (PDPL)

    Transfers abroad allowed under the transfer regulation, by adequacy or safeguards such as SCCs. Remote access from abroad can count as a transfer. Draft amendments would require storage in the Kingdom.

    Government & CNI data

    CST cloud rules keep government data in the Kingdom unless another law allows otherwise; CCC-2:2024 moved localisation to the wider national data rules.
